## Note
**The requirement (p. 17).** Employers must obtain each employee's name and SSN and enter both on Form W-2. An SSN has nine digits in the pattern 000-00-0000. Resident and nonresident alien employees are covered too. You should ask the employee to show their social security card, though the employee need not do so if the card is unavailable. When an employee can't provide the card, you should verify their SSN and their eligibility for employment as discussed later in the section under Verification of SSNs.

**Caution on the card (p. 17).** A card stating "Not valid for employment" must not be accepted, since an SSN issued with that legend doesn't permit employment. You may photocopy the card if the employee provides it, but you aren't required to. Without the correct employee name and SSN on Form W-2, you may owe a penalty unless you have reasonable cause. On the requirement to solicit the employee's SSN, the page points to Pub. 1586.

**Tip (p. 17).** Often a replacement social security card can be applied for online, with no visit to an SSA office. For some applicants an SSN application can be started online before a visit to an SSA office. The page points to SSA.gov/number-card.

**Applying for a social security card (p. 17).** An employee who is legally eligible to work in the United States but has no social security card can get one by completing Form SS-5 and submitting the necessary documentation. Form SS-5 is available from the SSA website at SSA.gov/forms/ss-5.pdf, at SSA offices, or by phone at 800-772-1213 or 800-325-0778 (TTY). The employee has to complete and sign it; the employer can't file it. If the employee has used up their yearly or lifetime limit on replacement cards, you may be asked to supply a letter with Form SS-5.

**Where to get and file Form SS-5 in the U.S. territories (pp. 17–18).** The page lists the U.S. SSA offices located in the U.S. territories (American Samoa, Guam, the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands and the U.S. Virgin Islands), each with an address. More information is on the Social Security Office Locator page at secure.ssa.gov/ICON, and the page adds that entering your ZIP code on that website finds your nearest SSA office in Puerto Rico.

**Applying for an SSN (p. 17).** Suppose you file Form W-2 on paper and an employee has applied for an SSN but has none when the form is due: enter "Applied For" on the form. For electronic filing, enter all zeros in the SSN field (000-00-0000 when creating forms online, 000000000 when uploading a file). Once the employee gets the SSN, file Copy A of Form W-2c with the SSA to show it, and give Copies B, C and 2 of Form W-2c to the employee. Online, up to 25 Forms W-2c per Form W-3c can be filed in a session, and the number of sessions is not limited. The page points to the SSA's Employer W-2 Filing Instructions & Information webpage at SSA.gov/employer. Advise the employee to correct the SSN on their original Form W-2.

**Correctly record the employee's name and SSN (p. 17).** Record each employee's name and SSN as shown on their social security card. When the name on the card is wrong (for example, after marriage or divorce), the employee should ask the SSA for an updated card. Keep reporting wages under the old name until the employee shows you the updated card with the corrected name. When the SSA issues an updated card after a name change, or issues a new card with a different SSN after a change in alien work status, file a Form W-2c correcting the name/SSN reported on the most recently filed Form W-2. Earlier years need no correction if the previous name and number were used for the years before the most recent Form W-2.

**IRS ITINs for aliens (pp. 17–18).** An ITIN must not be accepted in place of an SSN for identifying an employee or for work. ITINs are available only to resident and nonresident aliens who aren't eligible for U.S. employment and need identification for other tax purposes. An ITIN is a nine-digit number formatted like an SSN. It begins with "9", and its fourth and fifth digits fall in the ranges "50–65," "70–88," "90–92" or "94–99" (for example, 9NN-7N-NNNN). The page points to the Instructions for Form W-7 and to IRS.gov/ITIN for more information. A person with an ITIN who later becomes eligible to work in the United States must obtain an SSN. If that person is currently eligible to work in the United States, instruct them to apply for an SSN and follow the instructions under Applying for an SSN, earlier in the section. An ITIN is not to be used in place of an SSN on Form W-2 (p. 18).

**Verification of SSNs (p. 18).** Employers and authorized reporting agents can use the Social Security Number Verification Service (SSNVS) in two ways: to verify instantly that an employee name matches an SSN, for up to 10 names and SSNs (per screen) at a time, or to submit an electronic file of up to 250,000 names and SSNs and usually receive results the next business day. The page points to SSA.gov/employer/ssnv.htm. A person can hold a valid SSN without being authorized to work in the United States. To confirm the employment eligibility of newly hired employees, employers may use E-Verify at E-Verify.gov.

**Accessing the SSNVS (p. 18).** Access to the SSNVS goes through the SSA's BSO, and BSO users need a social security online account. An existing Login.gov credential or ID.me credential can be used; someone with neither will need to create one. The page points to SSA.gov/bso.

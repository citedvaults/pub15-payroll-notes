## Note
- If an employee submits an updated Form W-4, the employer has to use it, together with the Pub. 15-T withholding procedures, so the employee can reflect the expected qualified tips or qualified overtime deduction and get more pay in each paycheck (F03, p. 2).
- On new hire reporting, the page says that many states take a copy of Form W-4 with employer information added (F07, p. 7).
- Each new employee is asked to complete the 2026 Form W-4 (F07, p. 7).
- Federal income tax is withheld from each wage payment or supplemental unemployment compensation plan benefit payment according to the employee’s Form W-4 and the right withholding table in Pub. 15-T (F09, pp. 7–8).
- The Employer Responsibilities list includes asking new employees for Form W-4, withholding on each payday based on each employee’s Form W-4, reminding employees to turn in a new Form W-4 when they need to change their withholding, and asking for a new Form W-4 from employees who claim exemption (F09, p. 8).
- The records you keep should include copies of employees’ and recipients’ withholding certificates: Forms W-4, W-4P, W-4R, W-4S and W-4V (F11, p. 9).
- By February 15, the Calendar says to ask each employee who claimed exemption from withholding last year for a new Form W-4 (F21, p. 11).
- On February 16, an exemption Form W-4 for the previous year has expired. If the employee has not given a new Form W-4 for the current year, you start withholding as though the employee had checked Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c) and left Steps 2, 3 and 4 of the 2026 Form W-4 blank (F21, p. 11).
- A new Form W-4 claiming exemption that arrives after February 15 may be applied to future wages, but taxes withheld while the exempt status was not in place are not refunded (F21, p. 11).
- Before December 1, the Calendar says to remind employees to send a new Form W-4 when their filing status, other income, deductions or credits have changed or will change for the next year, and also when they made a mid-year change using the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator (F21, p. 12).
- An employee who made a mid-year change may be underwithheld or overwithheld once the Form W-4 applies for the next full calendar year (F21, p. 12).
- An employer need not withhold federal income tax on pay to an H-2A worker for agricultural labor under the visa, but may if the worker asks and the employer agrees; the worker then has to give the employer a completed Form W-4 (S05, p. 19).
- The part of an employee’s supplemental wages above $1 million in a calendar year is withheld at 37% (or the highest income tax rate for the year), without regard to the employee’s Form W-4 (S07, p. 24).
- The IRS redesigned Form W-4 for 2020 and later years. Because personal and dependency exemptions can no longer be claimed, the form no longer asks for a number of withholding allowances (S09.01, p. 25).
- The redesigned form has five steps. Steps 1 and 5 apply to every employee: Step 1 asks for personal information such as name and filing status, and Step 5 is the signature (S09.01, p. 25).
- An employee who completes only Steps 1 and 5 has withholding figured from the standard deduction and tax rates for the filing status, with no other adjustments (S09.01, p. 25).
- Step 2 lets an employee with other jobs in the household raise withholding for higher tax rates, either with an extra amount per payroll period in Step 4(c) or by checking the Step 2(c) box for higher withholding rate tables (S09.01, p. 25).
- Step 3 lowers withholding by the annual amount of credits the employee will claim, and Step 4 raises or lowers it based on the annual amount of other income or deductions and lets the employee request extra withholding per pay period (S09.01, pp. 25–26).
- An employee who gave you a Form W-4 before 2020 does not have to submit a new one merely because of the redesign; you keep figuring withholding from the most recent form (S09.01, p. 26).
- Newly hired employees have to use the redesigned form, and so do other employees who want to change their withholding (S09.01, p. 26).
- You should have a Form W-4 on file for every employee; ask new employees for a signed Form W-4 when they start work, and make it effective with the first wage payment (S09.01, p. 26).
- Where a new employee, including a rehired one, doesn’t give you a completed Form W-4 in 2026, you treat that employee as having checked Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c) and made no entries in Steps 2, 3 and 4 of the 2026 Form W-4 (S09.01, p. 26).
- You may set up a system for receiving Forms W-4 electronically from employees (S09.01, p. 26).
- A Form W-4 from 2025 or earlier stays in effect for 2026 unless the employee gives you a 2026 form, and when a new form arrives you do not change withholding for earlier pay periods (S09.01, p. 26).
- When a replacement Form W-4 comes in, withholding on it starts no later than the start of the first payroll period ending on or after the 30th day from the date you received it (for exceptions, the page points to Exemption from federal income tax withholding, IRS review of requested Forms W-4, and Invalid Forms W-4), and a form that changes things for the next calendar year takes no effect in the current year (S09.01, p. 26).
- You may suggest that employees use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator to help fill in their Forms W-4 (S09.01, p. 26).
- Withholding must rest on filing status, income (including income from other jobs), deductions and credits; employees can’t choose a fixed dollar amount or percentage, but may name an extra dollar amount to withhold each pay period (S09.01, p. 26).
- Married employees filing jointly whose spouses also work, and employees with more than one job, should allow for the higher tax rate by completing Step 2 of their 2026 Form W-4 (S09.01, pp. 26–27).
- Don’t accept withholding or estimated tax payments from employees on top of the withholding based on their Form W-4; an employee who needs more withholding should submit a new Form W-4 (S09.01, p. 27).
- In general, an employee may claim exemption from withholding having had no income tax liability last year and expecting none this year; the wages remain subject to social security and Medicare taxes (S09.01, p. 27).
- An exemption Form W-4 takes effect when given to the employer and lasts only for that calendar year, and the employee must give a new Form W-4 by February 15 to stay exempt (S09.01, p. 27).
- Nonresident aliens completing Form W-4 must ask for withholding as if single, write “Nonresident Alien” or “NRA” below Step 4(c), and may not claim exemption from withholding (S09.01, p. 27).
- A nonresident alien employee claiming a tax treaty exemption submits Form 8233 for the treaty-exempt income rather than Form W-4 (S09.01, p. 27).
- On request, you must let an IRS employee inspect original Forms W-4, and you may be told to send certain Forms W-4 to the IRS (S09.01, p. 27).
- After an IRS notice or modification notice, a new Form W-4 that claims complete exemption or leads to less withholding than the notice is disregarded unless the IRS tells you to use it, while a new form leading to more withholding is used (S09.01, p. 28).
- You are encouraged to have employees use the official Form W-4. A substitute must contain language identical to the official form, must meet all current IRS rules for substitute forms, and must be accompanied by all the current form’s tables, instructions and worksheets (S09.01, p. 28).
- Substitutes developed by employees are not accepted; one handed in after October 10, 2007 counts as no Form W-4 at all, but valid employee-developed forms accepted before October 11, 2007 stay honored (S09.01, p. 28).
- Any unauthorized change or addition makes a Form W-4 invalid, as does removing the language by which the employee certifies it is correct (S09.01, pp. 28–29).
- A Form W-4 is also invalid if the employee clearly indicates by the date they give it to you that it is false, and a false form may bring the employee a $500 penalty (S09.01, p. 29).
- You may treat a Form W-4 as invalid if the employee wrote “exempt” below Step 4(c) and also checked the Step 2(c) box or entered numbers for Steps 3 and 4 (S09.01, p. 29).
- On receiving an invalid Form W-4, don’t use it for withholding; tell the employee and ask for another. If none valid comes back, withhold as before when you hold an earlier valid form; otherwise withhold as for Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c) with no entries in Steps 2 to 4 of the 2026 form, except for an employee paid wages in 2019 who never submitted a valid Form W-4, who is still treated as single with zero allowances on a 2019 Form W-4 (S09.01, p. 29).
- Employers needn’t tell employees who claim exemption from withholding on Form W-4 about the EIC (S10, p. 31).

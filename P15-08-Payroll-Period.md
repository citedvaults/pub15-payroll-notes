## Note
**Payroll period (p. 25).** A payroll period is a period of service for which you usually pay wages. With a regular payroll period, income tax is withheld for that period even when the employee doesn't work all of it.

**No regular payroll period (p. 25).** Without a regular payroll period, withhold as though the wages were paid for a daily or miscellaneous payroll period. Count the days (Sundays and holidays included) in the period that the wage payment covers. When the wages are unrelated to a specific length of time (commissions paid on completion of a sale, for example), count back the number of days from the payment period to the latest of:
- The last wage payment made during the same calendar year.
- The date employment began, if during the same calendar year.
- January 1 of the same year.

**Employee paid for a period of less than 1 week (p. 25).** Suppose you pay an employee for less than 1 week, and the employee signs a statement under penalties of perjury saying that they are not working for any other employer during the same week for wages subject to withholding. Withholding is then figured on a weekly payroll period. If the employee later starts work for another employer for wages subject to withholding, the employee must tell you within 10 days, and withholding is then figured on the daily or miscellaneous period.

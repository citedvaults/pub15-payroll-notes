## Note
**Child employed by parents (p. 16).** Payments for a child's services are not subject to social security and Medicare taxes when the child is under age 18, works for their parent in a trade or business, and the business is a sole proprietorship or a partnership whose partners are all parents of the child. If the work is not in a trade or business (domestic work in the parent's private home is the page's example), the payments escape social security and Medicare taxes until the child reaches age 21. The page refers to Covered services of a child or spouse, later. For a child under age 21 working for their parent, in a trade or business or not, the payments are not subject to FUTA tax. Income tax withholding generally applies to payments for a child of any age working for their parent. The exceptions are domestic work in the parent's home, and work outside a trade or business that either pays less than $50 in the quarter or is work the child is not regularly employed to do.

**One spouse employed by another (p. 16).** Wages for an individual's services to their spouse in a trade or business are subject to income tax withholding and to social security and Medicare taxes, but not to FUTA tax. Payments for one spouse's services to the other outside a trade or business, such as domestic service in a private home, are not subject to social security, Medicare or FUTA taxes.

**Covered services of a child or spouse (p. 16).** Income tax withholding, social security, Medicare and FUTA taxes all apply to the wages for a child's or spouse's services when they work for any of these:
- A corporation, even one controlled by the child's parent or the individual's spouse.
- A partnership, even if the child's parent is a partner, unless every partner is a parent of the child.
- A partnership, even if the individual's spouse is a partner.
- An estate, even the estate of a deceased parent.

In these situations the child or spouse is considered to work for the corporation, partnership or estate, not for you.

**Parent employed by their child (p. 16).** The rules apply when the employer is a child who employs their parent:
- Payments for a parent's services in the child's (the employer's) trade or business are subject to income tax withholding and to social security and Medicare taxes.
- A parent's services outside the child's (the employer's) trade or business: payments are generally not subject to social security and Medicare taxes.

**Domestic services subject to social security and Medicare taxes (p. 16).** These taxes do apply to payments to a parent for domestic services when every one of the following holds:
- The employer is the parent's child.
- A child or stepchild of the employer (an adopted child included) lives in the home.
- The employer is a surviving spouse, or is divorced and not remarried, or lives with a spouse whose mental or physical condition prevents caring for the child or stepchild for at least 4 continuous weeks in the calendar quarter of the service.
- The employer's child or stepchild is under age 18, or needs an adult's personal care for at least 4 continuous weeks in the calendar quarter of the service because of a mental or physical condition.

Whatever services are provided, payments to a parent employed by their child are not subject to FUTA tax (p. 17).

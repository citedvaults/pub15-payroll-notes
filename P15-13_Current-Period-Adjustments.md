## Note
In certain cases the amounts reported as social security and Medicare taxes must be adjusted to arrive at the correct tax liability, for example by excluding amounts withheld by a third-party payer or amounts the employer was not required to withhold (p. 40). The amounts concerned are those in column 2 of Form 941, lines 5a–5d; on Form 943, lines 3, 5 and 7; and in column 2 of Form 944, lines 4a–4d. Current period adjustments are reported on Form 941, lines 7–9; Form 943, line 10; or Form 944, line 6 (p. 40). The page lists four types.

**Fractions-of-cents adjustment (pp. 40–41).** A small difference between total taxes after adjustments and nonrefundable credits (Form 941, line 12; Form 943, line 13; Form 944, line 9) and total deposits (Form 941, line 13; Form 943, line 14; Form 944, line 10) may be caused, in whole or in part, by rounding to the nearest cent each time payroll is figured. The rounding occurs when the social security and Medicare taxes to be withheld and deposited are figured from each employee's wages, and the IRS calls such differences "fractions-of-cents" adjustments. An employer that pays its taxes with Form 941 (or Form 943 or Form 944) instead of depositing, because total taxes for the quarter (the year for Form 943 or Form 944) are under $2,500, may also report a fractions-of-cents adjustment. To find out whether there is one for 2026, multiply the total wages and tips for the quarter that are subject to each of these taxes by the rate shown:
- social security tax reported on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944: 6.2% (0.062);
- Medicare tax reported on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944: 1.45% (0.0145);
- Additional Medicare Tax reported on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944: 0.9% (0.009).

Compare the results (the employee share of social security and Medicare taxes) with the total social security and Medicare taxes actually withheld from employees and shown in the payroll records for the quarter (Form 941) or the year (Form 943 or Form 944). A small difference, positive or negative, may be a fractions-of-cents adjustment. It is reported on Form 941, line 7; Form 943, line 10; or Form 944, line 6. If the amount actually withheld is less, report a negative adjustment with a minus sign if possible (otherwise in parentheses); if it is more, report a positive adjustment (p. 41).

**Adjustment of tax on third-party sick pay (p. 41).** Report both the employer share and the employee share of social security and Medicare taxes for sick pay on Form 941, lines 5a and 5c (Form 943, lines 2 and 4; Form 944, lines 4a and 4c). When the wages that the employer and the third-party payer paid to an employee together exceed $200,000 for the calendar year, the Additional Medicare Tax is reported on Form 941, line 5d (Form 943, line 7; Form 944, line 4d). Show the social security and Medicare taxes that a third-party payer withheld on sick pay as a negative adjustment on Form 941, line 8 (Form 943, line 10; Form 944, line 6). The page refers to section 6 of Pub. 15-A.

**Adjustment of tax on tips (p. 41).** If, by the 10th of the month after the month the employer received an employee's report on tips, there are not enough employee funds available to withhold the employee's share of social security and Medicare taxes, the employer no longer has to collect it. The entire amount of these tips is still reported on Form 941, lines 5b and 5c (Form 944, lines 4b and 4c). If the aggregate wages and tips paid to an employee exceed $200,000 for the calendar year, report the Additional Medicare Tax on Form 941, line 5d (Form 944, line 4d). The total uncollected employee share of social security and Medicare taxes goes in as a negative adjustment on Form 941, line 9 (Form 944, line 6).

**Adjustment of tax on group-term life insurance premiums paid for former employees (p. 41).** For premiums on group-term life insurance over $50,000 for a former employee, the former employee pays the employee share of social security and Medicare taxes with their own tax return, and the employer does not collect it. All social security and Medicare taxes for such coverage are still included on Form 941, lines 5a and 5c (Form 944, lines 4a and 4c). For Form 943, the social security wages and Medicare wages go on lines 2 and 4, and the social security tax and Medicare tax on lines 3 and 5. If the premium amount combined with other wages exceeds $200,000 for the calendar year, report the Additional Medicare Tax on Form 941, line 5d (Form 944, line 4d); for Form 943, the Additional Medicare Tax wages go on line 6 and the tax on line 7. The employee share of these taxes is backed out as a negative adjustment on Form 941, line 9 (Form 943, line 10; Form 944, line 6). The page refers to Pub. 15-B for more on group-term life insurance.

**Supporting statement (p. 41).** For these adjustments, the employer prepares and keeps a brief supporting statement explaining the nature and amount of each. The statement is not attached to Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944. The page refers to the General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 for reporting on Form W-2 the uncollected employee share of social security and Medicare taxes on tips and group-term life insurance.

**Example (p. 41).** Cedar, Inc., filed Form 941 and was entitled to these current period adjustments:
- Fractions of cents: over the quarter, Cedar, Inc. withheld and deposited a net $1.44 more in social security and Medicare taxes than the employee share of the column 2 amount on Form 941, lines 5a–5d. Adding or dropping fractions of cents on each wage payment caused the difference. Cedar, Inc. reports a positive $1.44 fractions-of-cents adjustment on Form 941, line 7.
- Third-party sick pay: Cedar, Inc. included $2,000 of taxes for sick pay in column 2 of Form 941, lines 5a and 5c. The third-party payer of the sick pay withheld and paid the employee share ($1,000) of these taxes, so Cedar, Inc. is entitled to a negative $1,000 sick pay adjustment on Form 941, line 8.
- Life insurance premiums: Cedar, Inc. paid group-term life insurance premiums for policies over $50,000 for former employees. The former employees owe the employee share ($200) of the social security and Medicare taxes on those policies, yet Cedar, Inc. includes that share in the column 2 taxes on Form 941, lines 5a and 5c. It is therefore entitled to a negative $200 adjustment on Form 941, line 9.

**No change to the record of federal tax liability (p. 41).** Current period adjustments leave the employer's record of federal tax liability as it is: Form 941, line 16, or Schedule B (Form 941); for Form 943 filers, Form 943, line 17, or Form 943-A; for Form 944 filers, Form 944, line 13, or Form 945-A. The record shows the actual amounts withheld from employees' wages for social security and Medicare taxes. The current period adjustments bring the amounts reported on Form 941, lines 5a–5d, column 2 (Form 943, lines 3, 5 and 7; Form 944, lines 4a–4d, column 2) into line with the actual amounts withheld, which are the amounts on the record, so no further change to the record is needed for these adjustments.

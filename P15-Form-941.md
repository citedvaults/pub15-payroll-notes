## Note
- Return transcripts for Forms 940, 941, 943, 944 and 945 for tax years 2023 and later are now available through your IRS business tax account (F03, p. 3).
- The COVID-19 related sick and family leave wage credit applies only to leave taken after March 31, 2020, and before October 1, 2021, and Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 can’t any longer be used to claim it (F04, p. 3).
- For tax periods beginning after 2023 the claim lines were removed from Form 941, Employer’s QUARTERLY Federal Tax Return, and from Forms 943 and 944. An employer entitled to the credit for wages paid after 2023 for an earlier applicable leave period claims it on Form 941-X (or Form 943-X or Form 944-X) (F04, pp. 3–4).
- A Form 941-X filed before the Form 941 for the quarter may cause errors or processing delays (F04, p. 4).
- The amount from Form 8974, line 12 or, if applicable, line 17, is reported on Form 941 (or Form 943 or Form 944); for reducing your record of tax liability for the payroll tax credit, the page points to the line 16 instructions in the Instructions for Form 941 (F04, p. 4).
- Approved section 3504 agents and CPEOs must complete Schedule R (Form 941), Allocation Schedule for Aggregate Form 941 Filers, for an aggregate Form 941 (F04, p. 4).
- An aggregate quarterly Form 941 is filed by an agent the IRS approved under section 3504, and also by CPEOs approved under section 7705 (F04, p. 4).
- CPEOs generally must file Form 941 or Form 943, with the applicable Schedule R, electronically (F04, p. 5).
- Other third-party payers filing an aggregate quarterly Form 941 or annual Form 943, non-certified PEOs for example, file the applicable Schedule R when their clients claim an employment tax credit (F04, p. 5).
- An employer that has filed quarterly Forms 941 and expects calendar-year employment taxes of $1,000 or less, and wants Form 944 instead, must contact the IRS in the first calendar quarter of the tax year and must receive written IRS notice before filing Form 944 in place of quarterly Forms 941 (F04, p. 5).
- Employers may also ask to file quarterly Forms 941 instead of an annual Form 944: an employer that got IRS notice to file Form 944 but prefers Forms 941 contacts the IRS in the first calendar quarter of the tax year, and may file quarterly only after receiving written IRS notice (F04, p. 5).
- A mistake on a filed Form 941 is corrected with Form 941-X, which is filed on its own and separately from Form 941 (F04, p. 5).
- Unless you have filed a “final” Form 940 and a “final” Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, or are a “seasonal” employer (Form 941 only), you continue to file Forms 941 for periods with no wages paid; the IRS encourages filing “zero wage” returns electronically (F04, p. 6).
- The Employer Responsibilities list includes filing Form 941, with payment of the tax with the return when no deposit is required, among the items under Quarterly (by April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31) (F09, p. 8).
- Payroll withholding (Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944) and nonpayroll withholding (Form 945) are deposited separately (F10, p. 8).
- Income tax withheld on distributions to participants from some nonqualified pension and deferred compensation plans goes on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, not on Form 945; distributions from such plans to a beneficiary or estate of a deceased employee aren't wages, and the income tax withheld on them goes on Form 945 (F10, pp. 8–9).
- The filing address for Form 941 generally turns on where your residence or principal place of business is located and on whether a payment goes with the return; tax-exempt organizations and government entities have separate addresses, found in the separate instructions for Form 941 (F14, pp. 9–10).
- For depositing taxes reported on Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 and Form 945, the Calendar refers to section 11 (F21, p. 10).
- Under the Calendar’s By January 31 date, Form 941 for the previous calendar year’s fourth quarter is filed and any income, social security and Medicare taxes not yet deposited are deposited; they may be paid with Form 941 when the quarter’s total tax liability (Form 941, line 12) is under $2,500 (F21, p. 11).
- Timely depositors of all taxes may file by February 10; for further rules on paying with the return, the page points to Payment with return in section 11 (F21, p. 11).
- Under the Calendar’s April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31 dates, Form 941 is filed and undeposited taxes are deposited; they may be paid with the form when the quarter’s total tax liability (Form 941, line 12) is under $2,500, and timely depositors of all taxes may file by May 10, August 10, November 10 or February 10, respectively (F21, p. 12).
- A business notified to file Form 944 that did not ask for, and receive, written IRS notice to file quarterly Forms 941 does not file Form 941 for those quarters (F21, p. 12).
- If an EIN you used on Form 941 (or Form 943 or Form 944) differs from the EIN reported on Form W-3, the page refers to Box h—Other EIN used this year in the General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 (S01, p. 13).
- Form 941 or Form 944 is filed to report withholding and employment taxes on tips (S06, p. 23).
- Tips and the collected and uncollected social security and Medicare taxes go on Form 941, lines 5b and 5c and, if applicable, 5d, and a negative adjustment for the uncollected taxes goes on Form 941, line 9 (S06, p. 23).
- A payment may accompany a timely filed Form 941 in place of a deposit, without penalty, when the Form 941 total tax liability (line 12) for the current or the prior quarter is under $2,500 and no $100,000 next-day deposit obligation arose in the current quarter (S11, p. 31).
- If you can’t be sure the current quarter’s liability will be under $2,500 (and the prior quarter’s was not), deposit under the semiweekly or monthly rules to avoid an FTD penalty (S11, p. 31).
- Deposits for Forms 941 (or 943 or 944) and for Form 945 are kept apart and never combined (S11, pp. 31–32).
- An employer of both farm and nonfarm workers treats Form 943 taxes for farmworkers separately from Form 941/944 taxes for nonfarm workers and makes separate deposits (S11, p. 32).
- The deposit schedule depends on the total tax liability reported on Form 941, line 12 (and on the other forms the page lists) during a lookback period (S11.01, p. 32).
- A Form 941 filer’s schedule for a calendar year comes from the taxes on Forms 941, line 12, over a 4-quarter lookback period running from July 1 to June 30: $50,000 or less makes a monthly schedule depositor, and more than $50,000 a semiweekly schedule depositor (S11.01, p. 32).
- The 2026 lookback period for a Form 941 filer that filed Form 944 in 2024 or 2025 is calendar year 2024 (S11.01, p. 32).
- Corrections made on Form 941-X do not change the tax liability counted for earlier periods under the lookback rule (S11.01, p. 32).
- A Form 941 filer whose lookback taxes (Forms 941, line 12, for the 4 quarters) were $50,000 or less is a monthly schedule depositor for the calendar year, and one whose taxes were more than $50,000 is a semiweekly schedule depositor (S11.01.01, p. 33; S11.01.02, p. 33).
- Monthly schedule depositors shouldn’t file Form 941 monthly (S11.01.01, p. 33).
- For Form 941 filers, liability for any lookback quarter before the business began or was acquired counts as zero, so a new filer is a monthly schedule depositor in its first calendar year; however, the page points to the $100,000 Next-Day Deposit Rule (S11.01.01, p. 33).
- A semiweekly schedule depositor completes Schedule B (Form 941), Report of Tax Liability for Semiweekly Schedule Depositors, and submits it with Form 941 (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- A Form 941 filer with several pay dates in one semiweekly period that fall in different calendar quarters makes separate deposits for the separate liabilities (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- Determining your schedule includes adding the taxes reported on Forms 941, line 12 (and on the other forms’ lines the page lists) for the lookback period (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- In the example, Rose Co. reported Form 941 taxes totalling $48,000 for its 2025 lookback period, so it is a monthly schedule depositor for 2025, and $51,000 for its 2026 lookback period (third quarter 2024 through second quarter 2025), so it will be a semiweekly schedule depositor for 2026 (S11.01.03, pp. 33–34).
- When you deposited too much for a quarter, you choose on Form 941 for that quarter whether the overpayment is refunded or credited to your next return (S11.02, p. 36).
- The deposit penalty information is written about Form 941 but applies to Forms 943, 944 and 945 as well (S11.03, p. 36).
- An employer that filed Form 944 for the prior year and files Forms 941 for the current year owes no FTD penalty for a late January deposit of the current year’s employment taxes if the taxes are fully deposited by March 15 of the current year (S11.03, p. 36).
- To avoid an “averaged” FTD penalty, a monthly schedule depositor lists tax liabilities (not deposits) in the monthly entry spaces on Form 941, line 16, and a semiweekly schedule depositor lists them on Schedule B (Form 941); check that the total liability on Form 941, line 16, or at the bottom of Schedule B (Form 941), equals the liability on Form 941, line 12 (S11.03, p. 37).
- An employer that paid wages subject to federal income tax withholding (sick pay and supplemental unemployment benefit withholding included) or social security and Medicare taxes files Form 941 every quarter, even with no taxes to report, unless it filed a final return, was notified it may file Form 944, or the exceptions on the page apply (S12, p. 38).
- An employer required to file Forms 941 that expects calendar-year employment taxes of $1,000 or less must contact the IRS in the first calendar quarter of the tax year to ask to file Form 944, and needs written IRS notice before filing it (S12, p. 38).
- Form 941 is due by the last day of the month after the quarter ends; with timely deposits fully paying the quarter’s taxes, it may be filed by the 10th day of the 2nd month after the quarter ends (S12, p. 38).
- Seasonal employers, household employers and agricultural employers are exceptions to the filing requirements for Form 941 and Form 944 (S12, p. 38).
- Seasonal employers needn’t file Forms 941 for quarters without tax liability because no wages were paid; they check the “Seasonal employer” box on Form 941, line 18, and the box at the top of the form for the quarter reported (S12, p. 38).
- The IRS generally won’t ask about unfiled returns when at least one taxable return is filed each year, but the “Seasonal employer” box must be checked on every Form 941 filed, or the IRS expects a return for each quarter (S12, p. 38).
- An employer that files Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 for business employees may add the taxes of household employees to that return, and agricultural employers report their social security, Medicare and withheld income taxes on Form 943 (S12, p. 38).
- Railroad employers report RRTA taxes on Form CT-1, and report federal income taxes withheld from their employees’ wages on Form 941 or Form 944 (S12, p. 38).
- Form 941 can be filed through the IRS e-file program, and reporting agents filing it for groups of taxpayers can file electronically (S12, p. 38).
- CPEOs, apart from the first quarter (Form 941 only) for which a CPEO is certified, must file Form 941 with Schedule R (Form 941) electronically (S12, p. 38).
- An employer with multiple locations or divisions files only one Form 941 per quarter, and a Form 941 should not report more than 1 calendar quarter (S12, p. 39).
- Name and EIN on Form 941 should read exactly as on earlier returns, and the Instructions for Form 941 explain how to prepare the form (S12, p. 39).
- A business that closes files a final return for the last quarter in which wages are paid (S12, p. 39).
- An employer required to file a final return also furnishes Forms W-2 to its employees and files Forms W-2 and W-3 with the SSA by the final return’s due date, and sends no original or copy of Form 941, 943 or 944 to the SSA (S12, p. 39).
- A late Form 941 for a previous year is filed on a copy of the form (with separate instructions) with a revision date showing the year and, if applicable, the quarter; the page gives IRS.gov/Form941 for prior year and quarterly forms (S12, p. 39).
- Bonuses are reported as wages and as social security and Medicare wages on Forms W-2 and on Forms 941, 943 or 944 (S12, p. 40).
- Social security and Medicare wages and taxes are reported separately on Forms W-2 and W-3 and on Forms 941, 943 or 944, and Form W-3 is reconciled with your four quarterly Forms 941 (S12, p. 40).
- Backup withholding and withholding on nonpayroll payments such as pensions, annuities and gambling winnings are not reported on Forms 941, 943 or 944, and only taxes and withholding that appear on Form W-2 belong on them (S12, p. 40).
- Where Additional Medicare Tax was withheld, the Medicare tax on Form 941, line 5c, column 2, will not be double the Medicare tax withheld in box 6 of Form W-3, because that tax falls on the employee alone (S12, p. 40).
- Certain social security and Medicare amounts reported on Form 941, lines 5a–5d, column 2, have to be adjusted to reach the correct tax liability, and current period adjustments are reported on Form 941, lines 7–9 (S13, p. 40).
- Fractions-of-cents adjustments go on Form 941, line 7, and an employer paying with Form 941 rather than depositing, because the quarter’s total taxes are under $2,500, may also report one (S13.01, pp. 40–41).
- Under the adjustment of tax on third-party sick pay, the employer share and the employee share of social security and Medicare taxes on sick pay are reported on Form 941, lines 5a and 5c; the Additional Medicare Tax goes on line 5d when the employer’s and third-party payer’s combined wages for an employee exceed $200,000 for the calendar year; and the taxes a third-party payer withheld on sick pay are a negative adjustment on line 8 (S13.01, p. 41).
- If, by the 10th of the month after an employee’s tip report was received, the employer lacks the employee funds to withhold the employee’s social security and Medicare share, it no longer has to collect it. The entire amount of those tips still goes on Form 941, lines 5b and 5c; the Additional Medicare Tax goes on line 5d if the employee’s wages and tips exceed $200,000 for the calendar year; and the total uncollected employee share is a negative adjustment on line 9 (S13.01, p. 41).
- For group-term life insurance premiums over $50,000 paid for a former employee, the former employee pays the employee share of the taxes with their own return and the employer does not collect it, yet all social security and Medicare taxes for the coverage go on Form 941, lines 5a and 5c; if the premium amount combined with other wages exceeds $200,000 for the calendar year, the Additional Medicare Tax goes on line 5d, and the employee share is backed out as a negative adjustment on line 9 (S13.01, p. 41).
- The supporting statement for these adjustments is not attached to Form 941 (S13.01, p. 41).
- In the example, Cedar, Inc., filed Form 941 and reports a positive $1.44 fractions-of-cents adjustment on line 7, a negative $1,000 sick pay adjustment on line 8, and a negative $200 adjustment on line 9 for the former employees’ share of taxes on group-term life insurance premiums (S13.01, p. 41).
- For current period adjustments you make no change to your record of federal tax liability on Form 941, line 16, or Schedule B (Form 941) (S13.01, p. 41).
- An error found on a filed Form 941 is corrected on a separate Form 941-X for each Form 941 corrected, and Form 941-X is filed on its own, not with Form 941 (S13.02, p. 42).
- Treasury Decision 9405 changed how interest-free adjustments to employment taxes reported on Form 941 are made and how a refund claim is filed (S13.02, p. 42).
- Income tax withheld in a prior calendar year can’t be adjusted unless the adjustment corrects an administrative error or section 3509 applies; an administrative error is an amount entered on Form 941, 943 or 944 that differs from the amount actually withheld (S13.02, p. 42).
- Where you did not repay or reimburse the employee, you report and pay the excess when you file the Form 941 for the quarter in which too much tax was withheld (S13.02, p. 43).
- FUTA tax for household employees need not be deposited unless their wages are reported on Form 941, 943 or 944; where they weren’t reported there, FUTA tax for them goes on Schedule H (Form 1040) (S14, pp. 44–45).
- A footnote to the section’s table says wages for services not considered farmwork are reported on Forms 941 or Form 944, and that other exemptions may apply (S15, p. 46).
- Where an employer and a section 3504 authorized agent (or CPEO or other third-party payer) both paid wages to an employee in a quarter, each should file Form 941 for the wages it paid that employee in the quarter (S16, p. 54).
- A CPEO files Schedule R (Form 940), Schedule R (Form 941) or Schedule R (Form 943), matching the aggregate return it files, which is Form 940, Form 941 or Form 943 respectively (S16, p. 54).
- A certification’s amounts must agree with the federal income tax withheld and reported on the agency’s quarterly federal tax return(s) (Form 941) (B03, p. 56).
- Return transcripts for Form 941 for tax years 2023 and later can be accessed through your IRS business tax account (B04, p. 57).

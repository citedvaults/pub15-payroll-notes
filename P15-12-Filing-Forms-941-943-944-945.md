## Note
Section 12 covers who files Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 and Form 945, when each is due, how they may be filed electronically, the penalties for filing or paying late, and how to reconcile Forms W-2 and W-3 with the quarterly and annual returns.

**Form 941 (p. 38).** An employer who paid wages subject to federal income tax withholding (including withholding on sick pay and supplemental unemployment benefits) or social security and Medicare taxes must file Form 941 each quarter, even with no taxes to report. The exceptions are a final return, an IRS notification that the employer may file Form 944, and the exceptions discussed later in the section. An employer required to file Forms 941 who expects employment taxes for the calendar year to be $1,000 or less, and would like to file Form 944 instead, must contact the IRS during the first calendar quarter of the tax year to ask for it. Form 944 may be filed in place of Forms 941 only after written notice from the IRS; the Instructions for Form 944 give the methods and deadlines for a request. Form 941 is due by the last day of the month after the quarter ends. An employer who made timely deposits in full payment of the quarter's taxes may file by the 10th day of the 2nd month after the quarter ends. The page points to the Calendar, earlier in the publication.

**Form 943 (p. 38).** Form 943 must be filed for each calendar year, starting with the first year in which you pay $2,500 or more for farmwork or employ a farmworker who meets the $150 test explained under Social Security and Medicare Taxes in section 9. These wages are not reported on Form 941, Form 944 or Form 945. The 2025 Form 943 is due February 2, 2026; with timely deposits in full payment of the year's taxes, it may be filed by February 10, 2026.

**Form 944 (p. 38).** An employer who receives written notification that it qualifies for the Form 944 program must file Form 944 instead of Forms 941. It must be filed even with no taxes to report (or with taxes over $1,000 to report), unless a final return was filed for the prior year. An employer notified to file Form 944 who prefers Forms 941 can request, during the first calendar quarter of the tax year, to have its filing requirement changed to Forms 941; the Instructions for Form 944 give the methods and deadlines. The 2025 Form 944 is due February 2, 2026; with timely deposits in full payment of the year's taxes, it may be filed by February 10, 2026.

**Form 945 (p. 38).** An employer who withholds, or is required to withhold, federal income tax (including backup withholding) from nonpayroll payments must file Form 945. All federal income tax withholding from nonpayroll payments reported on Forms 1099 or Form W-2G must go on Form 945 and not on another form such as Form 1040-ES. Withholding that must be reported on Form 1042 is not reported on Form 945. Form 945 need not be filed for years with no nonpayroll tax liability. The 2025 Form 945 is due February 2, 2026; with timely deposits in full payment of the year's taxes, it may be filed by February 10, 2026.

**Exceptions (p. 38).** These exceptions apply to the filing requirements for Form 941 and Form 944.
- Seasonal employers need not file Forms 941 for quarters with no tax liability because they paid no wages. To alert the IRS that no return will be filed for 1 or more quarters during the year, check the "Seasonal employer" box on Form 941, line 18, and check the box at the top of the form for the quarter reported. Generally, the IRS won't inquire about unfiled returns if at least one taxable return is filed each year, but the "Seasonal employer" box must be checked on every Form 941 filed; otherwise the IRS expects a return for each quarter.
- Household employers reporting social security and Medicare taxes and/or withheld income tax: an employer filing Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 for business employees may include the taxes for household employees on that return. Otherwise those taxes and the income tax withholding are reported on Schedule H (Form 1040). The page refers to Pub. 926.
- Agricultural employers report social security, Medicare and withheld income taxes on Form 943.

**Railroad employers (p. 38).** Employers that pay Railroad Retirement Tax Act (RRTA) taxes use Form CT-1 for the employment taxes the RRTA imposes. They use Form 941 or Form 944 for federal income taxes withheld from their employees' wages and other compensation.

**E-file (p. 38).** The IRS e-file program lets a taxpayer file Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 and Form 945 electronically. The page gives a web address and a telephone number for more information.

**Electronic filing by reporting agents (p. 38).** Reporting agents who file Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 or Form 945 for groups of taxpayers can file them electronically. The page refers to Pub. 3112 for details, to Revenue Procedure 2007-40 for information on electronic filing, and to section 16 for the different types of third-party payer arrangements.

**Electronic filing by CPEOs (pp. 38–39).** Except for the first quarter (Form 941 only) for which a CPEO is certified, CPEOs must file Form 941 electronically with Schedule R (Form 941) or, if applicable, Form 943 with Schedule R (Form 943). In certain circumstances the IRS may waive the electronic filing requirement. To ask for a waiver, the CPEO must send a written request through the IRS Online Registration System for Professional Employer Organizations at least 45 days before the due date of the return it cannot file electronically. The page refers to Revenue Procedure 2023-18.

**Penalties (p. 39).** For each whole or part month a return is not filed when required, the failure-to-file (FTF) penalty is 5% of the unpaid tax due with that return, generally up to a maximum of 25% of the tax due. For each whole or part month the tax is paid late, the failure-to-pay (FTP) penalty is 0.5% per month of the amount of tax, also up to a maximum of 25%. For individual filers only, the FTP penalty falls to 0.25% per month if an installment agreement is in effect, and only if the return was filed on or before its due date. If both penalties apply in a month, the FTF penalty is reduced by the amount of the FTP penalty. The penalties are not charged if there is reasonable cause for failing to file or pay, and a person who receives a penalty notice can explain why reasonable cause exists. Interest accrues from the due date of the tax on any unpaid balance, in addition to any penalties.

**Trust fund recovery penalty (p. 39).** A person may be personally liable for the trust fund recovery penalty if income, social security or Medicare tax that must be withheld is not withheld or is not paid. The page points to Trust fund recovery penalty in section 11.

**Third-party payers (p. 39).** Generally, using a third-party payer such as a PSP or reporting agent does not relieve an employer of responsibility for ensuring that returns are filed and taxes are paid or deposited correctly and on time. The page points to Certified professional employer organization (CPEO) in section 16 for an exception.

**One return per return period (p. 39).** Do not file more than one return per return period. An employer with multiple locations or divisions files only one Form 941 per quarter or one Form 944 per year. An agricultural employer files only one Form 943 per year. A payer of nonpayroll payments that withheld federal income tax or backup withholding files only one Form 945 per year. Filing more than one return may cause processing delays and may require correspondence with the IRS. For adjustments to previously filed returns, the page points to section 13.

**Reminders about filing (p. 39).**
- Don't report more than 1 calendar quarter on a Form 941.
- A needed Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 or Form 945 is available at IRS.gov/Forms; the page also points to Ordering Employer Tax Forms, Instructions, and Publications, earlier.
- Enter your name and EIN on the form exactly as they appeared on earlier returns.
- The Instructions for each of the four forms give information on preparing it.

**Final return (p. 39).** An employer who goes out of business must file a final return for the final period in which it pays wages: the last quarter, or the last year for Form 943, Form 944 or Form 945, where Form 945 looks to nonpayroll payments rather than wages. An employer who keeps paying wages or other compensation for periods after the business ends must file returns for those periods. The Instructions for each form give details on filing a final return. An employer required to file a final return must also furnish Forms W-2 to its employees and file Forms W-2 and W-3 with the SSA by the due date of the final return. An original or copy of Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 is not sent to the SSA. The page refers to the General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3, and employers in Puerto Rico to the General Instructions for Forms W-3 (PR) and W-3C (PR).

**Filing late returns for previous years (p. 39).** To file a delinquent return, get a copy of Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 or Form 945 (and its separate instructions) with a revision date that shows the year and, if applicable, the quarter being filed. Prior year and quarterly versions are available at the IRS.gov addresses the page gives for each form (select the link for "All form revisions"). The page also points to Ordering Employer Tax Forms, Instructions, and Publications, earlier, and gives a telephone number to call the IRS about late returns.

**Reconciling Forms W-2 and W-3 with Forms 941, Form 943, or Form 944 (pp. 39–40).** When Forms 941, 943 or 944 filed with the IRS differ from Forms W-2 and W-3 filed with the SSA, the IRS or the SSA may contact the employer to resolve the differences. The page lists these steps to help reduce them:
1. Report bonuses as wages and as social security and Medicare wages on Forms W-2 and on Forms 941, 943 or 944.
2. Report social security and Medicare wages and taxes separately on Forms W-2 and W-3 and on Forms 941, 943 or 944.
3. Put the employee share of social security taxes in the Form W-2 box for social security tax withheld (box 4) and not in social security wages; on Form 499R-2/W-2PR that tax goes in box 21.
4. Put the employee share of Medicare taxes in the Form W-2 box for Medicare tax withheld (box 6) and not in Medicare wages; on Form 499R-2/W-2PR that tax goes in box 23.
5. Make sure each employee's social security wages do not exceed the annual social security wage base limit ($184,500 for 2026).
6. Noncash wages not subject to social security or Medicare tax, covered earlier under Wages not paid in money in section 5, must not be reported as social security or Medicare wages.
7. When the EIN used on any Forms 941, 943 or 944 for the year is not the one on Form W-3, the other EIN goes in the Form W-3 box labelled "Other EIN used this year" (box h, or box f on Form W-3 (PR)).
8. Make sure the amounts on Form W-3 are the total of the amounts from Forms W-2.
9. Reconcile Form W-3 with the four quarterly Forms 941, the annual Form 943 or the annual Form 944 by comparing federal income tax withheld, social security and Medicare wages, and social security and Medicare taxes. Generally the taxes shown on Forms 941, 943 or 944, with current year adjustments, should be approximately twice the amounts on Form W-3, because those returns report both the employer and employee social security and Medicare taxes while Form W-3 reports only the employee taxes.

Backup withholding and withholding on nonpayroll payments, such as pensions, annuities and gambling winnings, are not reported on Forms 941, 943 or 944. Withholding on nonpayroll payments goes on Forms 1099 or Form W-2G, and the employer must report it on Form 945. Forms 941, 943 and 944 carry only the taxes and withholding that are reported on Form W-2.

Amounts on Forms W-2, W-3 and Forms 941, 943 or 944 may fail to match for valid reasons. For example, where Additional Medicare Tax was withheld from an employee's wages, the Medicare tax on Form 941 (line 5c, column 2), Form 943 (line 5) or Form 944 (line 4c, column 2) will not be twice the Medicare tax withheld in box 6 of Form W-3 (box 13 of Form W-3 (PR)), because Additional Medicare Tax is imposed only on the employee and has no employer share. The employer should make sure there are valid reasons for any mismatch and keep the reconciliation as a record of why amounts did not match, in case the IRS or the SSA inquires. The page points to the Instructions for Schedule D (Form 941) for explaining discrepancies caused by an acquisition, statutory merger or consolidation.

## Note
**Caution (p. 43).** Employers in American Samoa, Guam and the CNMI do not owe FUTA tax, but employers in the USVI and Puerto Rico do. Work done for a federally recognized Indian tribal government is exempt from FUTA tax, as is work for any subdivision, subsidiary or wholly owned business of such a tribe, subject to the tribe's compliance with state law. The page refers to section 3309(d) and to Pub. 4268.

**The tax (p. 43).** The Federal Unemployment Tax Act (FUTA) works with state unemployment systems to pay unemployment compensation to workers who have lost their jobs. Most employers pay a federal and also a state unemployment tax. The page refers to the U.S. Department of Labor's website for a list of state unemployment agencies. Only the employer pays FUTA tax; it is not withheld from the employee's wages. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 940.

**Who must pay (pp. 43–44).** Three tests decide whether FUTA tax must be paid. Each applies to a different category of employee and each is independent of the others. If a test describes the employer's situation, the employer is subject to FUTA tax on the wages it pays to employees in that category during the current calendar year.
1. General test: for wages paid to employees who are not farmworkers or household workers, the employer is subject to FUTA tax in 2026 if either (a) it paid wages of $1,500 or more in any calendar quarter of 2025 or 2026, or (b) it had at least one employee, for some part of a day, in each of 20 or more different weeks of 2025 or of 20 or more different weeks of 2026.
2. Household employees test: the employer is subject to FUTA tax in 2026 if it paid total cash wages of $1,000 or more to household employees in any calendar quarter of 2025 or 2026. A household employee does household work in a private home, a local college club, or a local fraternity or sorority chapter.
3. Farmworkers test: for wages paid to farmworkers, the employer is subject to FUTA tax in 2026 if either (a) it paid cash wages of $20,000 or more to farmworkers in any calendar quarter of 2025 or 2026, or (b) it employed 10 or more farmworkers, for some part of a day and not necessarily at the same time, in each of 20 or more different weeks of 2025 or of 20 or more different weeks of 2026.

Under either farmworker test, the employer must count wages paid to aliens admitted to the United States on a temporary basis to perform farmwork (H-2A visa workers). Wages paid to H-2A visa workers are nevertheless not subject to FUTA tax (p. 44). Farmworkers supplied by crew leaders, as defined earlier in section 2, are generally treated as employees of the farm operator for FUTA tax. The exceptions are where (a) the crew leader is registered under the Migrant and Seasonal Agricultural Worker Protection Act, or (b) substantially all the workers the crew leader supplies operate or maintain tractors, harvesting or crop-dusting machines, or other machines the crew leader provides. If (a) or (b) applies, the farmworkers are generally employees of the crew leader (p. 44).

**Figuring FUTA tax (p. 44).** For 2026 the FUTA tax rate is 6.0%. The tax applies to the first $7,000 paid to each employee as wages during the year, which is the federal wage base; the state wage base limit may be different. Generally, a credit against FUTA tax is allowed for amounts paid into state unemployment funds, and it may be as much as 5.4% of FUTA taxable wages. An employer with the full 5.4% credit has a FUTA tax rate of 0.6% after the credit. The maximum credit goes to an employer that paid its state unemployment taxes in full, on time, and on every wage that is also subject to FUTA tax, provided the state is not determined to be a credit reduction state. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 940 to determine the credit.

In some states, state unemployment tax covers the very wages that FUTA tax covers. Certain states, though, leave some kinds of wages out of state unemployment tax although FUTA tax applies to them: the page's examples are wages paid to corporate officers, certain payments of sick pay by unions, and certain fringe benefits. For such wages the employer may be required to deposit more than 0.6% FUTA tax. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 940 (p. 44).

**Successor employer (p. 44).** An employer that acquired a business from an employer who was liable for FUTA tax may be able to count the wages that employer paid to the employees who continue to work for it when figuring the $7,000 FUTA tax wage base limit. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 940.

**Depositing FUTA tax (p. 44).** For deposit purposes, FUTA tax is figured quarterly. The liability is the taxable wages paid during the quarter multiplied by 0.6%, an amount that may need adjusting depending on entitlement to the credit for state unemployment contributions. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 940. Deposits of FUTA tax on an employee's wages stop once taxable wages reach $7,000 for the calendar year. If the FUTA tax liability for a calendar quarter is $500 or less, no deposit is required; the amount may be carried forward and added to the liability figured in the next quarter to see whether a deposit must be made. If the liability for a calendar quarter is over $500 (including any FUTA tax carried forward from an earlier quarter), the tax must be deposited by EFT. The page points to section 11 for more on EFTs.

**Household employees (p. 44).** FUTA taxes for household employees need not be deposited unless their wages are reported on Forms 941, Form 943 or Form 944. The page refers to Pub. 926.

**When to deposit (p. 44).** The FUTA tax is deposited by the last day of the first month after the end of the quarter. If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the deposit may be made on the next business day. The page points to Legal holiday in section 11 for a list of the legal holidays occurring in 2026. If the liability for the fourth quarter (plus any undeposited amount from any earlier quarter) is over $500, the entire amount is deposited by the due date of Form 940 (January 31). If it is $500 or less, the employer can make a deposit, pay the tax with a credit or debit card, or pay the tax with its Form 940 by January 31. An employer that files Form 940 electronically can e-file and use EFW to pay the balance due. The page refers to IRS.gov/Pay for paying by credit or debit card or using EFW.

**Caution (p. 44).** In years when there are credit reduction states, liabilities owed for credit reduction must be included with the fourth quarter deposit. The anticipated extra liability may be deposited throughout the year, but it is not due until the due date for the fourth quarter deposit, and the associated liability should be recorded as incurred in the fourth quarter. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 940.

Table 4. When To Deposit FUTA Taxes (p. 44)

| Quarter months | Quarter ending | Deposit due date |
|---|---|---|
| Jan.–Feb.–Mar. | Mar. 31 | Apr. 30 |
| Apr.–May–June | June 30 | July 31 |
| July–Aug.–Sept. | Sept. 30 | Oct. 31 |
| Oct.–Nov.–Dec. | Dec. 31 | Jan. 31 |

**Reporting FUTA tax (pp. 44–45).** Form 940 is used to report FUTA tax. The 2025 Form 940 is due February 2, 2026; an employer that deposited all FUTA tax when due may file on or before February 10, 2026.

**Form 940 e-file (p. 45).** The Form 940 e-file program lets a taxpayer file an original Form 940 and an amended Form 940 electronically.

**Electronic filing by reporting agents (p. 45).** Reporting agents who file Forms 940 for groups of taxpayers can file them electronically. The page points to Electronic filing by reporting agents in section 12.

**Electronic filing by CPEOs (p. 45).** CPEOs must file Form 940 electronically with Schedule R (Form 940). In certain circumstances the IRS may waive the electronic filing requirement. To ask for a waiver, the CPEO must send a written request through the IRS Online Registration System for Professional Employer Organizations at least 45 days before the due date of the return it cannot file electronically. The page refers to Revenue Procedure 2023-18.

**Household employees (p. 45).** An employer that did not report employment taxes for household employees on Forms 941, Form 943 or Form 944 reports FUTA tax for them on Schedule H (Form 1040), and must have an EIN to file Schedule H (Form 1040). The page refers to Pub. 926.

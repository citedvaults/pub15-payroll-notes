## Note
- Return transcripts for Forms 940, 941, 943, 944 and 945 for tax years 2023 and later are now available through your IRS business tax account (F03, p. 3).
- Federal income tax withheld on nonpayroll payments (reported on Forms 1099 and Form W-2G) is reported on Form 945, Annual Return of Withheld Federal Income Tax (F10, p. 8).
- Payroll withholding (Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944) and nonpayroll withholding (Form 945) require separate deposits (F10, p. 8).
- Nonpayroll items include pensions (distributions from tax-favored retirement plans too) and annuities, IRA distributions, military retirement, gambling winnings, Indian gaming profits, and payments subject to backup withholding, among others the page lists; details on depositing and reporting are in the Instructions for Form 945 (F10, p. 8).
- Distributions to participants from some nonqualified pension and deferred compensation plans are treated as wages, so the income tax withheld goes on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 and not on Form 945 (F10, pp. 8–9).
- Distributions from such plans to the beneficiary or estate of a deceased employee are not wages; they are reported on Forms 1099-R, and the income tax withheld on them goes on Form 945 (F10, p. 9).
- The Employer Responsibilities list includes filing Form 945 for any nonpayroll income tax withholding among the items under Annually (see Calendar for due dates) (F09, p. 8).
- The filing address for Form 945 generally turns on where your residence or principal place of business is located and on whether a payment goes with the return (F14, p. 9).
- Tax-exempt organizations and government entities have separate filing addresses, which are in the separate instructions for Form 945 (F14, pp. 9–10).
- Under the Calendar’s By January 31 date, Form 945 reports any nonpayroll federal income tax withheld; if all taxes were deposited when due, it may be filed by February 10 (F21, p. 11).
- The name and EIN on Form 945 have to match those on your information returns that report federal income tax withholding, such as backup withholding shown on Form 1099-NEC (S01, pp. 13–14).
- An incorrect EIN, or another business’s EIN, on Form 945 can lead to penalties and delays in processing the return (S01, p. 14).
- An employer must backup withhold when an H-2A worker gives no TIN and the worker’s total annual wages are at least $600 in 2025 ($2,000 in 2026); the page refers to the Instructions for Form 945 (S05, p. 19).
- A payment may accompany a timely filed Form 941, 943, 944 or 945 in place of a deposit, without penalty, in the cases the page lists; for Form 945 that case is annual total tax liability (Form 945, line 3) under $2,500 (S11, p. 31).
- If you aren’t sure you will report under $2,500 on Form 945, deposit under the section’s rules to avoid an FTD penalty (S11, p. 31).
- Deposits for Forms 941 (or 943 or 944) and for Form 945 are kept apart and never combined (S11, pp. 31–32).
- Nonpayroll deposit rules are generally the same as the payroll rules, except that the return period is a year, not a quarter; if the year’s total tax reported on Form 945 is under $2,500, no deposits are required during the year (S11, p. 32).
- A Form 945 filer’s deposit schedule for a calendar year comes from the total taxes on line 3 of its Form 945 for the second preceding calendar year, so the lookback period for 2026 is calendar year 2024 (S11.01, p. 32).
- For Form 945 filers, a lookback total (Form 945, line 3) of $50,000 or less makes a monthly schedule depositor, and more than $50,000 a semiweekly schedule depositor (S11.01.01, p. 33; S11.01.02, p. 33).
- For Form 945 filers, liability from before the business began or was acquired counts as zero in the lookback period, so a new filer is a monthly schedule depositor in the first and second calendar years; however, the page points to the $100,000 Next-Day Deposit Rule (S11.01.01, p. 33).
- A semiweekly schedule depositor filing Form 945 completes Form 945-A, Annual Record of Federal Tax Liability, and submits it with the return (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- For Form 945 filers the return period is a calendar year (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- Employment taxes, including Form 945 taxes, are deposited by EFT (S11.02, p. 35).
- If you deposited too much, the Form 945 for that year is where you choose a refund or a credit toward your next return (S11.02, p. 36).
- The deposit penalty information is written about Form 941 but applies to Form 945 as well (S11.03, p. 36).
- Before submitting Form 945, check that the total liability on Form 945, line 7, or Form 945-A, line M, equals the tax liability on Form 945, line 3, and show no negative amounts on Form 945, line 7, or Form 945-A (S11.03, p. 37).
- A taxpayer who withholds, or must withhold, federal income tax (backup withholding included) from nonpayroll payments files Form 945 (S12, p. 38).
- Withholding on nonpayroll payments reported on Forms 1099 or Form W-2G goes on Form 945 and not on another form such as Form 1040-ES (S12, p. 38).
- Withholding that belongs on Form 1042 is not reported on Form 945, and Form 945 need not be filed for years without nonpayroll tax liability (S12, p. 38).
- Your 2025 Form 945 is due by February 2, 2026, or by February 10, 2026 if timely deposits fully paid the year’s taxes (S12, p. 38).
- Farmwork wages are not reported on Form 941, Form 944 or Form 945 (S12, p. 38).
- Form 945 can be filed through the IRS e-file program, and reporting agents filing it for groups of taxpayers can file electronically (S12, p. 38).
- Only one Form 945 per year is filed by a payer of nonpayroll payments that withheld federal income tax or backup withholding (S12, p. 39).
- Name and EIN on Form 945 should read exactly as on earlier returns, and the Instructions for Form 945 explain how to prepare the form (S12, p. 39).
- A business that closes files a final return for the last year in which nonpayroll payments are paid (S12, p. 39).
- A late Form 945 for a previous year is filed on a copy of the form with a revision date showing that year (S12, p. 39).
- Withholding on nonpayroll payments such as pensions, annuities and gambling winnings is reported on Form 945, not on Forms 941, 943 or 944 (S12, p. 40).
- Return transcripts for Form 945 for tax years 2023 and later can be accessed through your IRS business tax account (B04, p. 57).

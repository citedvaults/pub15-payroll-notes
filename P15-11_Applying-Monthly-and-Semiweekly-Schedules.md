## Note
The labels monthly schedule depositor and semiweekly schedule depositor say nothing about how often a business pays its employees or even how often it must deposit. They name the set of deposit rules to follow when an employment tax liability arises. Those rules look at the dates wages are paid (the cash basis), not at when liabilities accrue for accounting purposes.

**Monthly schedule example.** Spruce Co., a monthly schedule depositor with seasonal employees, paid wages every Friday in March and none in April. Under the monthly schedule it must deposit the combined liabilities for the March paydays by April 15. It has no deposit requirement for April (which would be due by May 15), since it paid no wages and so had no tax liability for April.

**Semiweekly schedule example.** Green, Inc., a semiweekly schedule depositor, pays wages once a month, on the last Friday. It deposits only once a month, since it has wages only once a month, but the deposit follows the semiweekly schedule: the liability for the Friday, May 29, 2026 payday must be deposited by Wednesday, June 3, 2026. Under that schedule, liabilities for wages paid Wednesday through Friday are deposited by the following Wednesday.

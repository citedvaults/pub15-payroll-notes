## Note
How To Get Tax Help lists IRS resources for answers to tax questions, forms and publications, tax return preparers, language and accessibility services, payments, and responding to notices, and it ends with a message from the Taxpayer Advocate Service (TAS). The chapter's own text is summarised below. The message from TAS has four parts, which follow in the cluster.

**Where to start (p. 56).** For questions about a tax issue, help preparing a tax return, or free publications, forms and instructions, the page says to go to IRS.gov for resources that can help right away.

**Tax reform (p. 56).** Tax reform legislation affecting federal taxes, credits and deductions was enacted in P.L. 119-21. IRS.gov/OBBB has more information and updates on how it affects your taxes.

**Preparing and filing your tax return (p. 56).** IRS.gov/EmploymentEfile has more information on filing employment tax returns electronically.

**Getting answers to your tax questions (p. 56).** IRS.gov has up-to-date information on current events and changes in tax law. IRS.gov/Help offers a variety of tools for answers to some of the most common tax questions. IRS.gov/Forms has forms, instructions and publications, with details on the most recent tax changes and interactive links to help find answers. Tax information may also be accessible in your e-filing software.

**Need someone to prepare your tax return? (p. 56).** There are various types of tax return preparers, including enrolled agents, certified public accountants (CPAs), accountants, and many others without professional credentials, and the page says to choose a preparer wisely. A paid tax preparer is primarily responsible for the overall substantive accuracy of your return, is required to sign the return, and is required to include their preparer tax identification number (PTIN). The Caution says that although the preparer always signs the return, you are ultimately responsible for providing all the information the preparer needs to prepare it accurately and for the accuracy of every item reported on it. Anyone paid to prepare returns for others should have a thorough understanding of tax matters. The page refers to Tips for Choosing a Tax Preparer on IRS.gov.

**Business Services Online and W-2 filing (p. 57).** Employers can register to use Business Services Online. The SSA offers an online service at SSA.gov/employer, described as fast, free and secure, for W-2 filing by CPAs, accountants, enrolled agents and individuals who process Form W-2 and Form W-2c.

**Business tax account (p. 57).** A sole proprietor, partnership, S corporation, C corporation or single-member limited liability company (LLC) can view its tax information on record with the IRS and do more with a business tax account. IRS.gov/BusinessAccount has more information.

**IRS social media (p. 57).** IRS.gov/SocialMedia lists the social media tools the IRS uses to share the latest information on tax changes, scam alerts, initiatives, products and services. The page says that at the IRS, privacy and security are the highest priority, and that these tools are used to share public information. Do not post your identification number (EIN or SSN) or other confidential information on social media sites, and always protect your identity when using any social networking site. Three IRS YouTube channels offer short videos on various tax-related topics in English, Spanish and ASL: Youtube.com/irsvideos, Youtube.com/irsvideosmultilingua and Youtube.com/irsvideosASL.

**Online tax information in other languages (p. 57).** IRS.gov/MyLanguage has information for those whose native language is not English.

**Over-the-Phone Interpreter (OPI) Service (p. 57).** Taxpayers who need language interpretation can use the IRS's OPI Service. Its locations are Taxpayer Assistance Centers (TACs), most IRS offices, and each Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA) and Tax Counseling for the Elderly (TCE) return site. The languages offered are Spanish, Mandarin, Cantonese, Korean, Vietnamese, Russian and Haitian Creole.

**Accessibility Helpline (p. 57).** Taxpayers with disabilities who need information about accessibility services can call 833-690-0598. The Helpline can answer questions about current and future accessibility products and services available in alternative media formats (for example, braille-ready, large print, audio, etc.). It does not have access to your IRS account. For help with tax law, refunds or account-related issues, the page refers to IRS.gov/LetUsHelp.

**Disasters (p. 57).** IRS.gov/DisasterRelief lists the available disaster tax relief.

**Getting tax forms and publications (p. 57).** IRS.gov/Forms is where most of the forms, instructions and publications needed can be viewed, downloaded or printed, and IRS.gov/OrderForms is where to place an order.

**Getting tax publications and instructions in eBook format (p. 57).** Most tax publications and instructions (including Pub. 15) can be downloaded and viewed on mobile devices as eBooks at IRS.gov/eBooks. The IRS eBooks have been tested using Apple's iBooks for iPad. They have not been tested on other dedicated eBook readers, and eBook functionality may not operate as intended.

**Get a transcript of your return (p. 57).** Return transcripts of Form 940, Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 and Form 945 for tax years 2023 and later can now be accessed through your IRS business tax account. The page refers to IRS.gov/BusinessTranscript and, for the business tax account, to IRS.gov/BusinessAccount.

**Using direct deposit (p. 57).** The page calls e-filing and choosing direct deposit the safest and easiest way to receive a tax refund; direct deposit securely and electronically transfers the refund into your financial account. Direct deposit also avoids the possibility that a check could be lost, stolen, destroyed or returned undeliverable to the IRS. Someone without a bank account can go to IRS.gov/DirectDeposit for where to find a bank or credit union that can open an account online.

**Reporting and resolving your tax-related identity theft issues (p. 57).**
- Tax-related identity theft means someone steals your personal information in order to commit tax fraud. If your EIN is used for a fraudulent return, or to claim a refund or credit, your taxes can be affected.
- The IRS does not start contact with taxpayers through email, text messages (shortened links included), telephone calls or social media channels to ask for or confirm personal or financial information. That covers requests for personal identification numbers (PINs), passwords or similar details for credit cards, banks or other financial accounts.
- IRS.gov/IdentityTheft, called the IRS Identity Theft Central webpage, carries information about identity theft and data security protection for taxpayers, tax professionals and businesses. A person whose EIN has been lost or stolen, or who suspects tax-related identity theft, can learn which steps to take.

**Making a tax payment (pp. 57–58).** The IRS recommends paying electronically whenever possible. Payments of U.S. tax must be remitted to the IRS in U.S. dollars, and digital assets are not accepted. IRS.gov/Pay has information on how to pay with any of these options (pp. 57–58):
- IRS Direct Pay: taxes are paid from your bank account. It is free and secure, needs no sign-in, and lets you change or cancel within 2 days of the scheduled payment.
- Debit Card, Credit Card or Digital Wallet: an approved payment processor takes the payment online or by phone.
- Electronic Funds Withdrawal: a payment is scheduled while you file your federal taxes, through tax return preparation software or a tax professional.
- Electronic Federal Tax Payment System: described as the best option for businesses; enrollment is required.
- Check or Money Order: the payment is mailed to the address on the notice or instructions.
- Cash: payment in cash may be possible at a participating retail store.
- Same-Day Wire: a same-day wire from your financial institution may be possible; ask the institution about availability, cost and time frames.

The page's Note says the IRS uses the latest encryption technology to ensure that electronic payments made online, by phone or from a mobile device using the IRS2Go app are safe and secure, and that paying electronically is quick and easy (p. 58).

**What if I can't pay now? (p. 58).** IRS.gov/Pay has more information about the options. A taxpayer who cannot pay in full today can apply for an online payment agreement (IRS.gov/OPA) to meet the tax obligation in monthly installments, and immediately learns whether the agreement has been approved once the online process is complete. The Offer in Compromise Pre-Qualifier shows whether a tax debt can be settled for less than the full amount owed; IRS.gov/OIC has more on the Offer in Compromise program.

**Understanding an IRS notice or letter (p. 58).** IRS.gov/Notices has additional information about responding to an IRS notice or letter.

**IRS Document Upload Tool (p. 58).** For eligible IRS notices and letters, a taxpayer may be able to reply digitally with the Document Upload Tool, which securely uploads the required documents online through IRS.gov. The page refers to IRS.gov/DUT.

**Contacting your local TAC (p. 58).** Many questions can be answered on IRS.gov without visiting a TAC, and IRS.gov/LetUsHelp covers the topics people ask about most. If you still need help, TACs provide tax help when a tax issue cannot be handled online or by phone. All TACs now provide service by appointment, so you know in advance that you can get the service you need without long wait times. Before visiting, go to IRS.gov/TAC to find the nearest TAC and to check hours, available services and appointment options. Alternatively, on the IRS2Go app, under the Stay Connected tab, choose the Contact Us option and click on "Local Offices."

**Message from the Taxpayer Advocate Service (p. 58).** The page introduces the message that follows as one from the Taxpayer Advocate Service, an independent organization established by Congress.

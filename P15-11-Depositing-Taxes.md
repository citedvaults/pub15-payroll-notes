## Note
Section 11 of the publication covers depositing federal employment taxes: when deposits are due, how to make them and the penalties for not depositing properly. The chapter's own opening text, summarised below, sets the general deposit rule, the cases where a payment may go with a return in place of a deposit, and the separate-deposit requirements; its parts follow in the clusters.

**General rule (p. 31).** Generally, withheld federal income tax and both the employer's and the employee's social security and Medicare taxes have to be deposited. Every federal tax deposit must be made by EFT, and the electronic deposit requirements are covered later in the section, under How To Deposit.

**Payment with return (p. 31).** A payment may go with a timely filed Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 or Form 945 in place of a deposit, with no penalty, in any of these cases:
- You are a monthly schedule depositor (a term defined later) and your payment follows the accuracy of deposits rule, discussed later in the section; such a payment may be $2,500 or more.
- On Form 941, the total tax liability (line 12) for the current quarter or for the prior quarter is less than $2,500, and no $100,000 next-day deposit obligation arose during the current quarter. If you are unsure that the current quarter's liability will be under $2,500 (and the prior quarter's liability was not under $2,500), the publication advises depositing under the semiweekly or monthly rules, to avoid an FTD penalty.
- On Form 943, the total tax liability for the year (line 13) is less than $2,500. If you are unsure that you will report under $2,500, the publication advises depositing under the section's rules, to avoid an FTD penalty.
- On Form 944, the total tax liability for the year (line 9) is less than $2,500; or it is $2,500 or more, you have already deposited the taxes owed for the first three quarters, the net tax for the fourth quarter is less than $2,500, and you pay that fourth-quarter tax in full with a timely filed return.
- On Form 945, the total tax liability for the year (line 3) is less than $2,500. If you are unsure that you will report under $2,500, the publication advises depositing under the section's rules, to avoid an FTD penalty.

**Nonpayroll (Form 945) tax liabilities (pp. 31–32).** Nonpayroll income tax withholding is deposited separately from payroll withholding, so liabilities for Form 945 are not combined with those for Form 941 (or Form 943 or Form 944) in one deposit. Generally, the nonpayroll deposit rules match the ones described next in the publication, except that the return period is a year, not a quarter. When the year's total tax reported on Form 945 is less than $2,500, no deposits are required during the year; the separate Instructions for Form 945 give more information.

**Employers of both farm and nonfarm workers (p. 32).** Employment taxes for farmworkers (Form 943 taxes) and for nonfarm workers (Form 941/944 taxes) must be treated separately. They are not combined when any of the deposit schedule rules is applied, and when a deposit is due each group is deposited on its own. For example, a monthly schedule depositor for both groups with an end-of-April liability of $1,500 reportable on Form 941/944 and $1,200 reportable on Form 943 deposits both amounts by May 15, in two transactions: one for the $1,500 and one for the $1,200.

## Note
**Forms for prior-period adjustments (pp. 41–42).** After an error is discovered on a previously filed Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, the correction is made with Form 941-X, Form 943-X or Form 944-X. Form 945-X and Form CT-1 X report corrections on the corresponding returns. Form 843 is used to request a refund or abatement of assessed interest or penalties. The page refers to Revenue Ruling 2009-39 for examples of how the interest-free adjustment and claim for refund rules apply in 10 different situations.

**Background (p. 42).** Treasury Decision 9405 changed how interest-free adjustments to employment taxes on Form 941, Form 943 and Form 944 are made, and how a claim for refund of employment taxes is filed. The adjustment process is used if employment taxes were underreported and a payment is being made, or if they were overreported and the credit will be applied to the Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 period in which Form 941-X, Form 943-X or Form 944-X is filed. The claim process is used if employment taxes were overreported and a refund or abatement of the overreported amount is requested. The page says that "correct" and "corrections" cover interest-free adjustments under sections 6205 and 6413 and claims for refund and abatement under sections 6402, 6414 and 6404.

**Correcting employment taxes (p. 42).** On discovering an error on a previously filed Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, an employer must:
- correct the error with Form 941-X, Form 943-X or Form 944-X;
- file a separate correction form of that kind for each return being corrected; and
- send each correction form on its own and not with Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944.

Current quarter adjustments for fractions of cents, third-party sick pay, tips and group-term life insurance are reported on Form 941 using lines 7–9, on Form 943 using line 10 and on Form 944 using line 6; the page points to Current Period Adjustments, earlier in the section. Corrections of underreported and overreported amounts for the same tax period go on a single Form 941-X, Form 943-X or Form 944-X, unless a refund is requested. If a refund is requested and both underreported and overreported amounts are being corrected, file one form correcting only the underreported amounts and a second form correcting only the overreported amounts. The chart on the last page of Form 941-X, Form 943-X or Form 944-X helps in choosing between the adjustment process and the claim process, and the instructions for each form give the details for making an adjustment or claim.

**E-file (p. 42).** The IRS e-file program lets a taxpayer file Form 941-X, Form 943-X and Form 945-X electronically.

**Income tax withholding adjustments (p. 42).** In a current calendar year, an error in income tax withholding for a prior quarter is corrected on Form 941-X when it is discovered. An adjustment may be made only for income tax withholding errors discovered in the same calendar year in which the wages were paid, because the employee claims the amount shown on Form W-2 (or, if applicable, Form W-2c) as a credit on their income tax return. Amounts reported as income tax withheld in a prior calendar year cannot be adjusted unless the adjustment corrects an administrative error or section 3509 applies. An administrative error occurs if the amount entered on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 is not the amount actually withheld, for example because of a mathematical or transposition error. The administrative error adjustment corrects the amount on the return to agree with the amount actually withheld from employees and reported on their Forms W-2.

**Additional Medicare Tax withholding adjustments (p. 42).** Generally, the rules for income tax withholding adjustments apply to Additional Medicare Tax withholding adjustments. An adjustment may be made for errors discovered in the same calendar year in which the wages were paid, and amounts reported in a prior calendar year cannot be adjusted unless the adjustment corrects an administrative error or section 3509 applies. An employer that overpaid Additional Medicare Tax cannot claim a refund of the overpayment unless the amount was not actually withheld from the employee's wages (which would be an administrative error). If a prior-year error was a nonadministrative error, the employer may correct only the wages and tips subject to Additional Medicare Tax withholding.

**Collecting underwithheld taxes from employees (p. 42).** If no income, social security or Medicare tax, or less than the correct amount, was withheld from an employee's wages, the employer can make it up from later pay to that employee. The employer still owes the underpayment, and reimbursement is a matter for settlement between the employer and the employee. Underwithheld income tax and Additional Medicare Tax must be collected from the employee no later than the last day of the calendar year. The page points to special rules for tax on tips (section 6) and for fringe benefits (section 5).

**Refunding amounts incorrectly withheld from employees (pp. 42–43).** If more than the correct amount of income, social security or Medicare tax was withheld from wages, the employer repays or reimburses the employee the excess. Any excess income tax or Additional Medicare Tax withholding must be repaid or reimbursed before the end of the calendar year in which it was withheld. The employer keeps in its records the employee's written receipt showing the date and amount of the repayment, or a record of the reimbursement. If the employer did not repay or reimburse the employee, it must report and pay each excess amount when it files Form 941 for the quarter (or Form 943 or Form 944 for the year) in which too much tax was withheld.

**Correcting filed Forms W-2 and W-3 (p. 43).** When adjustments correct wages and social security and Medicare taxes because of a change in the wage totals reported for a previous year, Form W-2c and Form W-3c must also be filed with the SSA. Up to 25 Forms W-2c per Form W-3c may be filed per online session, with no limit on the number of sessions. The page refers to the SSA's Employer W-2 Filing Instructions & Information webpage.

**Exceptions to interest-free corrections of employment taxes (p. 43).** A correction is not eligible for interest-free treatment if:
- the failure to report concerns an issue raised during an IRS examination of a prior return, or
- the employer underreported its employment tax liability knowingly.

A correction is also not eligible for interest-free treatment after the earlier of receipt of an IRS notice and demand for payment after assessment, and receipt of an IRS notice of determination under section 7436.

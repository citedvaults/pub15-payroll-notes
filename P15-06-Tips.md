## Note
**Reporting cash tips (p. 22).** Withholding generally applies to cash tips an employee gets from customers. The employee must report them to you by the 10th of the month following the month in which they were received. Tips paid by cash, check, debit card or credit card all count as cash tips. A report should cover tips you paid over to the employee for charge customers, tips received directly from customers, and tips received from other employees under a tip-sharing arrangement. Directly and indirectly tipped employees alike must report tips to you. For a month in which tips total less than $20, no report is required.

**The employee's statement (p. 22).** When you give your employees no specific reporting method (an electronic tip reporting system is the page's example), they must give you a statement of their tips, signed and dated by the employee. The statement has to contain:
- The employee's name, address and SSN.
- Your name and address.
- The month and year covered (or the beginning and ending dates, if the statement covers less than 1 calendar month).
- The total of tips received during that month or period.

You are permitted to set up a system for employees to report tips electronically; the page refers to Regulations section 31.6053-1(d). You may also suggest that employees see Pub. 531, Reporting Tip Income.

**Collecting taxes on tips (pp. 22–23).** Three taxes must be collected on an employee's tips: federal income tax (the page refers to Federal income tax withholding on tips, later), employee social security tax and employee Medicare tax. The rules for withholding the employee's share of Medicare tax on tips apply equally to the Additional Medicare Tax once wages and tips go above $200,000 in the calendar year. These taxes can come out of the employee's wages (not counting tips) or out of other funds the employee makes available; the page refers to Tips are treated as supplemental wages in section 7. The employee share of social security tax stops being collected when wages and tips for tax year 2026 reach $184,500, whereas income tax and employee Medicare tax are collected for the whole year on all wages and tips. Your own responsibility is the employer social security tax on wages and tips until they (tips included) reach the limit, and the employer Medicare tax on all wages and tips for the whole year. Tips count as paid when the employee reports them to you. Deposit taxes on tips according to your deposit schedule, as described in section 11 (pp. 22–23). Form 941 or Form 944 is the form for reporting withholding and employment taxes on tips (p. 23).

**Ordering rule (p. 23).** Suppose that by the 10th of the month after the month covered by an employee's tip report you lack enough employee funds to deduct the employee tax: you then no longer have to collect it. Where funds fall short, taxes are withheld in this sequence:
1. Regular wages and other compensation.
2. Social security and Medicare taxes on tips.
3. Income tax on tips.

**Federal income tax withholding on tips (p. 23).** P.L. 119-21 lets employees and self-employed individuals deduct on their income tax returns up to $25,000 of qualified tips, for tax years beginning after 2024 and ending before 2029. The tips must have been received in occupations that customarily and regularly received tips on or before December 31, 2024. Qualified tips are cash tips; these include voluntary cash or charged tips from customers and, for employees, tips received through tip-sharing arrangements. Mandatory service charges added to the bill are not qualified tips. Where an employee submits an updated Form W-4, employers must use it, along with the federal income tax withholding procedures in Pub. 15-T, so that the employee can account for the expected deduction and get more money in each paycheck instead of waiting for the full benefit until the income tax return is filed.

**Reporting tips on forms (p. 23).** Tips and any collected and uncollected social security and Medicare taxes go on Form W-2 (Form 499R-2/W-2PR for employers in Puerto Rico) and on Form 941, lines 5b and 5c and, if applicable, 5d (Form 944, lines 4b and 4c and, if applicable, 4d). A negative adjustment for the uncollected social security and Medicare taxes goes on Form 941, line 9 (Form 944, line 6). In box 12 of Form W-2 the uncollected social security tax is entered with code "A" and the uncollected Medicare tax with code "B." On Form 499R-2/W-2PR they go in boxes 25 and 26 respectively. Any uncollected Additional Medicare Tax stays out of box 12 of Form W-2. For more on reporting tips the page refers to section 13 and to the General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3; employers in Puerto Rico are referred to the General Instructions for Forms W-3 (PR) and W-3C (PR). Revenue Ruling 2012-18 gives employers guidance on social security and Medicare taxes imposed on tips, including reporting the employer share of these taxes under section 3121(q), the difference between tips and service charges, and the section 45B credit.

**FUTA tax on tips (p. 23).** FUTA tax also applies to tips when an employee reports $20 or more of them to you in writing in a month.

**Allocated tips (p. 23).** An operator of a large food or beverage establishment must report allocated tips in certain circumstances, but must not withhold income, social security or Medicare tax on them. The page defines a large food or beverage establishment as one in the 50 states or the District of Columbia that serves food or beverages for consumption on the premises, where tipping is customary, and that normally had more than 10 employees on a typical business day during the preceding year. Allocation may follow one of three methods: hours worked, gross receipts or good-faith agreement. For these methods, and for required electronic filing of Form 8027, the page refers to the Instructions for Form 8027; for filing Form 8027 electronically with the IRS it refers to Pub. 1239.

**Tip Rate Determination and Education Program (p. 23).** Employers may take part in this program, which is built mainly on two voluntary agreements meant to improve tip income reporting by helping taxpayers understand and meet their reporting responsibilities. They are the Tip Rate Determination Agreement (TRDA) and the Tip Reporting Alternative Commitment (TRAC). For the gaming (casino) industry there is a separate tip agreement, the Gaming Industry Tip Compliance Agreement (GITCA). The page refers to Pub. 3144 for more information.

**More information (p. 23).** The page advises telling employees to see Pub. 531, or to use the IRS Interactive Tax Assistant at IRS.gov/TipIncome, to find out whether their tip income is taxable and how to report it.

## Note
- Return transcripts for Forms 940, 941, 943, 944 and 945 for tax years 2023 and later are now available through your IRS business tax account (F03, p. 3).
- The COVID-19 related sick and family leave wage credit applies only to leave taken after March 31, 2020, and before October 1, 2021, and Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 can’t any longer be used to claim it (F04, p. 3).
- For tax periods beginning after 2023 the claim lines were removed from Form 944, Employer’s ANNUAL Federal Tax Return, and from Forms 941 and 943. An employer entitled to the credit for wages paid after 2023 for an earlier applicable leave period claims it on Form 944-X (or Form 941-X or Form 943-X) (F04, pp. 3–4).
- A Form 944-X filed before the Form 944 for the year may cause errors or processing delays (F04, p. 4).
- The amount from Form 8974, line 12 or, if applicable, line 17, is reported on Form 944 (or Form 941 or Form 943); for reducing your record of tax liability for the payroll tax credit, the page points to the line 13 instructions in the Instructions for Form 944 (F04, p. 4).
- Written notice from the IRS is required before you may file Form 944 (F04, p. 5).
- An employer that has filed quarterly Forms 941 and expects calendar-year employment taxes of $1,000 or less, and wants Form 944 instead, must contact the IRS in the first calendar quarter of the tax year and must receive written IRS notice before filing Form 944 in place of quarterly Forms 941 (F04, p. 5).
- Employers may also ask to file quarterly Forms 941 instead of an annual Form 944: an employer that got IRS notice to file Form 944 but prefers Forms 941 contacts the IRS in the first calendar quarter of the tax year, and may file quarterly only after receiving written IRS notice (F04, p. 5).
- For the methods and deadlines of either request, the page points to the Instructions for Form 944 (F04, p. 5).
- A mistake on a filed Form 944 is corrected with Form 944-X, which is filed on its own and separately from Form 944 (F04, p. 5).
- Unless you have filed a “final” Form 940 and a “final” Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, you continue to file Form 944 for periods with no wages paid (the “seasonal” employer condition is for Form 941 only); the IRS encourages filing “zero wage” returns electronically (F04, p. 6).
- The Employer Responsibilities list includes filing Form 944 if required, with payment of the tax with the return when no deposit is required, among the items under Annually (see Calendar for due dates) (F09, p. 8).
- Payroll withholding (Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944) and nonpayroll withholding (Form 945) are deposited separately (F10, p. 8).
- Income tax withheld on distributions to participants from some nonqualified pension and deferred compensation plans goes on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, not on Form 945; distributions from such plans to a beneficiary or estate of a deceased employee aren't wages, and the income tax withheld on them goes on Form 945 (F10, pp. 8–9).
- The filing address for Form 944 generally turns on where your residence or principal place of business is located and on whether a payment goes with the return; tax-exempt organizations and government entities have separate addresses, found in the separate instructions for Form 944 (F14, pp. 9–10).
- For depositing taxes reported on Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 and Form 945, the Calendar refers to section 11 (F21, p. 10).
- Under the Calendar’s By January 31 date, an employer the IRS has notified in writing files Form 944 for the previous calendar year in place of Form 941, and pays any income, social security and Medicare taxes not yet deposited with it (F21, p. 11).
- Those taxes may be paid with Form 944 when the year’s total tax liability (Form 944, line 9) is under $2,500; for further rules on paying with the return, the page points to Payment with return in section 11 (F21, p. 11).
- A business notified to file Form 944 that did not ask for, and receive, written IRS notice to file quarterly Forms 941 does not file Form 941 for the quarterly dates listed (F21, p. 12).
- If an EIN you used on Form 944 (or Form 941 or Form 943) differs from the EIN reported on Form W-3, the page refers to Box h—Other EIN used this year in the General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 (S01, p. 13).
- Form 941 or Form 944 is filed to report withholding and employment taxes on tips (S06, p. 23).
- Tips and the collected and uncollected social security and Medicare taxes go on Form 944, lines 4b and 4c and, if applicable, 4d, and a negative adjustment for the uncollected taxes goes on Form 944, line 6 (S06, p. 23).
- The deposit schedule depends on the total tax liability reported on Form 944, line 9 (and on the other forms the page lists) during a lookback period (S11.01, p. 32).
- For a Form 944 filer in the current year or either of the 2 years before, the schedule comes from the taxes reported in the second preceding calendar year; for 2026 the lookback period for a Form 943 or Form 944 filer is calendar year 2024 (S11.01, p. 32).
- A Form 944 filer whose lookback taxes (Form 944, line 9) were $50,000 or less is a monthly schedule depositor for the calendar year, and one whose taxes were more than $50,000 is a semiweekly schedule depositor (S11.01.01, p. 33; S11.01.02, p. 33).
- Monthly schedule depositors shouldn’t file Form 944 monthly (S11.01.01, p. 33).
- For Form 944 filers, liability from before the business began or was acquired counts as zero in the lookback period, so a new filer is a monthly schedule depositor in its first and second calendar years; however, the page points to the $100,000 Next-Day Deposit Rule (S11.01.01, p. 33).
- A semiweekly schedule depositor filing Form 944 completes Form 945-A, Annual Record of Federal Tax Liability, and submits it with the return (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- For annual Form 944 the return period is a calendar year (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- When you deposited too much, you choose on Form 944 for that year whether the overpayment is refunded or credited to your next return (S11.02, p. 36).
- The deposit penalty information is written about Form 941 but applies to Form 944 as well (S11.03, p. 36).
- An employer that filed Form 944 for the prior year and files Forms 941 for the current year owes no FTD penalty for a late January deposit of the current year’s employment taxes if the taxes are fully deposited by March 15 of the current year (S11.03, p. 36).
- To avoid an “averaged” FTD penalty, a monthly schedule depositor lists tax liabilities (not deposits) in the monthly entry spaces on Form 944, line 13, and a semiweekly schedule depositor lists them on Form 945-A; check that the total liability on Form 944, line 13, or Form 945-A, line M, equals the liability on Form 944, line 9 (S11.03, p. 37).
- With written notification that you qualify for the Form 944 program, you file Form 944 rather than Forms 941, even if you have no taxes to report (or have more than $1,000 to report), unless you filed a final return for the prior year (S12, p. 38).
- An employer notified to file Form 944 who prefers Forms 941 may ask, during the first calendar quarter of the tax year, to have the filing requirement changed back to Forms 941; the page points to the Instructions for Form 944 (S12, p. 38).
- Your 2025 Form 944 is due by February 2, 2026, or by February 10, 2026 if timely deposits fully paid the year’s taxes (S12, p. 38).
- An employer required to file Forms 941 that expects calendar-year employment taxes of $1,000 or less must contact the IRS in the first calendar quarter of the tax year to ask to file Form 944, and needs written IRS notice before filing it (S12, p. 38).
- Seasonal employers, household employers and agricultural employers are exceptions to the filing requirements for Form 941 and Form 944; an employer that files Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 for business employees may add the taxes of household employees to that return (S12, p. 38).
- Railroad employers report RRTA taxes on Form CT-1, and report federal income taxes withheld from their employees’ wages on Form 941 or Form 944 (S12, p. 38).
- Form 944 can be filed through the IRS e-file program, and reporting agents filing it for groups of taxpayers can file electronically (S12, p. 38).
- An employer with multiple locations or divisions files only one Form 944 per year (S12, p. 39).
- Name and EIN on Form 944 should read exactly as on earlier returns, and the Instructions for Form 944 explain how to prepare the form (S12, p. 39).
- A business that closes files a final return for the last year in which wages are paid (S12, p. 39).
- A late Form 944 for a previous year is filed on a copy of the form (with separate instructions) with a revision date showing that year; the page gives IRS.gov/Form944 for prior year forms (S12, p. 39).
- Social security and Medicare wages and taxes are reported separately on Forms W-2 and W-3 and on Forms 941, 943 or 944, and Form W-3 is reconciled with the annual Form 944 (S12, p. 40).
- Only taxes and withholding that appear on Form W-2 belong on Forms 941, 943 or 944 (S12, p. 40).
- Where Additional Medicare Tax was withheld, the Medicare tax on Form 944, line 4c, column 2, will not be double the Medicare tax withheld in box 6 of Form W-3, because that tax falls on the employee alone (S12, p. 40).
- Certain social security and Medicare amounts reported on Form 944, lines 4a–4d, column 2, have to be adjusted to reach the correct tax liability, and current period adjustments are reported on Form 944, line 6 (S13, p. 40).
- Fractions-of-cents adjustments go on Form 944, line 6, and an employer paying with Form 944 rather than depositing, because the year’s total taxes are under $2,500, may also report one (S13.01, pp. 40–41).
- Under the adjustment of tax on third-party sick pay, the employer share and the employee share of social security and Medicare taxes on sick pay are reported on Form 944, lines 4a and 4c; the Additional Medicare Tax goes on line 4d when the employer’s and third-party payer’s combined wages for an employee exceed $200,000 for the calendar year; and the taxes a third-party payer withheld on sick pay are a negative adjustment on line 6 (S13.01, p. 41).
- If, by the 10th of the month after an employee’s tip report was received, the employer lacks the employee funds to withhold the employee’s social security and Medicare share, it no longer has to collect it. The entire amount of those tips still goes on Form 944, lines 4b and 4c; the Additional Medicare Tax goes on line 4d if the employee’s wages and tips exceed $200,000 for the calendar year; and the total uncollected employee share is a negative adjustment on line 6 (S13.01, p. 41).
- For group-term life insurance premiums over $50,000 paid for a former employee, the former employee pays the employee share of the taxes with their own return and the employer does not collect it, yet all social security and Medicare taxes for the coverage go on Form 944, lines 4a and 4c; if the premium amount combined with other wages exceeds $200,000 for the calendar year, the Additional Medicare Tax goes on line 4d, and the employee share is backed out as a negative adjustment on line 6 (S13.01, p. 41).
- The supporting statement for these adjustments is not attached to Form 944 (S13.01, p. 41).
- For current period adjustments you make no change to your record of federal tax liability on Form 944, line 13, or Form 945-A (S13.01, p. 41).
- An error found on a filed Form 944 is corrected on a separate Form 944-X for each Form 944 corrected, and Form 944-X is filed on its own, not with Form 944 (S13.02, p. 42).
- Treasury Decision 9405 changed how interest-free adjustments to employment taxes reported on Form 944 are made and how a refund claim is filed (S13.02, p. 42).
- Income tax withheld in a prior calendar year can’t be adjusted unless the adjustment corrects an administrative error or section 3509 applies; an administrative error is an amount entered on Form 941, 943 or 944 that differs from the amount actually withheld (S13.02, p. 42).
- Where you did not repay or reimburse the employee, you report and pay the excess when you file the Form 944 for the year in which too much tax was withheld (S13.02, p. 43).
- FUTA tax for household employees need not be deposited unless their wages are reported on Form 941, 943 or 944; where they weren’t reported there, FUTA tax for them goes on Schedule H (Form 1040) (S14, pp. 44–45).
- A footnote to the section’s table says wages for services not considered farmwork are reported on Forms 941 or Form 944, and that other exemptions may apply (S15, p. 46).
- Return transcripts for Form 944 for tax years 2023 and later can be accessed through your IRS business tax account (B04, p. 57).

## Note
**Redesigned Form W-4 (pp. 25–26).** The IRS redesigned Form W-4 for 2020 and later years. Until 2020 the value of a withholding allowance was tied to the amount of the personal exemption. Since changes in the law mean taxpayers can no longer claim personal exemptions or dependency exemptions, Form W-4 no longer has the employee report a number of withholding allowances. The revised form has five steps:
- Steps 1 and 5 apply to all employees: in Step 1 employees give personal information such as name and filing status, and in Step 5 they sign. An employee who completes only those two steps gets withholding figured on the standard deduction and tax rates for their filing status, with no other adjustments.
- Step 2, where applicable, raises withholding to account for higher tax rates caused by income from other jobs in the household. Employees can either enter an additional amount to withhold per payroll period in Step 4(c) or tick the box in Step 2(c) so that higher withholding rate tables apply to their wages.
- Step 3 lowers withholding by the annual amount of any credits the employee will claim on their income tax return.
- Step 4 lets employees raise or lower withholding according to the annual amount of other income or deductions they will report on their income tax return, and also to request any additional federal income tax they want withheld each pay period.

An employee who submitted a Form W-4 in any year before 2020 need not submit a new one just because of the redesign, and employers keep figuring withholding from the information on the employee's most recently submitted Form W-4. The Pub. 15-T withholding tables let employers figure withholding from a Form W-4 for 2019 or earlier as well as from the redesigned form. An employee first paid wages before 2020 who hasn't yet submitted a redesigned form may be asked to submit one, but you should explain that submitting it is not required and that, without a new Form W-4, withholding continues on a valid Form W-4 submitted earlier. All newly hired employees must use the redesigned form, and so must any other employees who want to adjust their withholding (p. 26).

Pub. 15-T offers an optional computational bridge for treating 2019 and earlier Forms W-4 as if they were 2020 or later Forms W-4 when figuring federal income tax withholding. With the bridge you apply the computational procedures and data fields of a 2020 and later Form W-4 to reach the withholding that the procedures and data fields of a 2019 or earlier Form W-4 would have produced. The page refers to How To Treat 2019 and Earlier Forms W-4 as if They Were 2020 or Later Forms W-4 under Introduction in Pub. 15-T (p. 26).

**More information (p. 26).** On the redesigned Form W-4 and on regulations guiding employers on income tax withholding from employees' wages, the page refers to Treasury Decision 9924. It points to IRS.gov/FormW4 for information about Form W-4. Pub. 15-T, available at IRS.gov/Pub15T, holds the employer instructions on figuring employee withholding.

**Tip (p. 26).** Farm operators and crew leaders must withhold federal income tax from farmworkers' wages if those wages are subject to social security and Medicare taxes.

**Using Form W-4 to figure withholding (p. 26).** You should hold a Form W-4 for each employee in order to know how much federal income tax to withhold. Encourage employees to file an updated Form W-4 for 2026; this matters most for those who owed taxes, or got a large refund, on their 2025 tax return. New employees should be asked for a signed Form W-4 when they start work, and the form takes effect with the first wage payment. A new employee who doesn't give you a completed Form W-4 in 2026 (a rehired employee who fails to furnish one is included) is treated as though they had checked the box for Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c) and made no entries in Steps 2, 3 or 4 of the 2026 Form W-4. An employee paid wages before 2020 who failed to furnish a Form W-4 continues to be treated as single and claiming zero allowances on a 2019 Form W-4. With the optional computational bridge, described earlier under Redesigned Form W-4, you may instead treat this employee as having checked the box for Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c), made no entries in Steps 2 and 3, entered $8,600 in Step 4(a) and entered zero in Step 4(b) of the 2026 Form W-4.

**Electronic system to receive Form W-4 (p. 26).** You may set up a system for receiving Forms W-4 electronically from employees. The page refers to Regulations section 31.3402(f)(5)-1(c) and Pub. 15-T for more information.

**Effective date of Form W-4 (p. 26).** A Form W-4 for 2025 or an earlier year stays in effect for 2026 unless the employee gives you a 2026 Form W-4. Withholding for pay periods before the effective date of a new Form W-4 is not adjusted. A Form W-4 that replaces an existing one must be put into effect no later than the start of the first payroll period ending on or after the 30th day from the date you received the replacement. For exceptions the page refers to Exemption from federal income tax withholding, IRS review of requested Forms W-4 and Invalid Forms W-4, later in the section. A Form W-4 making a change for the next calendar year does not take effect in the current calendar year.

**Successor employer (p. 26).** A successor employer (the page refers to Successor employer, later in the section) should secure new Forms W-4 from the transferred employees, unless the "Alternative Procedure" in section 5 of Revenue Procedure 2004-53 applies. The page refers to Revenue Procedure 2004-53, 2004-34 I.R.B. 320.

**IRS Tax Withholding Estimator for employees (p. 26).** You may advise employees to use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator at IRS.gov/W4App to help decide how to complete their Forms W-4. An employee who changes their withholding mid-year after using the Estimator may be underwithheld or overwithheld once the Form W-4 applies to the next full calendar year. You should therefore remind such employees to return to the Estimator in early January and submit a new Form W-4 for the year.

**Completing Form W-4 (pp. 26–27).** Any federal income tax withholding has to rest on filing status, income (income from other jobs included), deductions and credits; employees may not base withholding on a fixed dollar amount or percentage. An employee may, however, specify a dollar amount to be withheld each pay period on top of the withholding based on filing status and the other information reported on Form W-4 (p. 26). Employees who are married filing jointly and whose spouses also currently work, and employees who hold more than one job at the same time, should account for their higher tax rate by completing Step 2 of the 2026 Form W-4. Employees can also report on their 2026 Form W-4 other income not subject to withholding and other deductions they will claim, which makes federal income tax withholding more accurate. The page refers to Pub. 505 for more on completing Form W-4 and says you may wish to order Pub. 505 for employees along with Form W-4. Accept no withholding or estimated tax payments from employees beyond the withholding based on their Form W-4. An employee needing additional withholding should submit a new Form W-4 and, if necessary, pay estimated tax by filing Form 1040-ES or making an electronic payment of estimated taxes. Employees who receive tips may provide funds to their employer for withholding on tips; the page refers to Collecting taxes on tips in section 6 (p. 27).

**Exemption from federal income tax withholding (p. 27).** Generally, an employee may claim exemption from federal income tax withholding on the ground of having had no income tax liability last year and expecting none this year; the page refers to the Form W-4 instructions. Social security and Medicare taxes still apply to the wages. The page also refers to Invalid Forms W-4, later in the section. A Form W-4 claiming exemption takes effect when the employer receives it and lasts only for that calendar year. To stay exempt, the employee must give a new Form W-4 by February 15. Without one by February 15, begin withholding as though the employee had checked the box for Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c) and made no entries in Steps 2, 3 or 4 of the 2026 Form W-4. A new exemption Form W-4 given on February 16 or later may be applied to future wages, but taxes withheld while the exempt status wasn't in place are not refunded.

**Withholding federal income taxes on the wages of nonresident alien employees (p. 27).** In general, you must withhold federal income taxes on nonresident alien employees' wages. The page refers to Pub. 515 for exceptions, and to section 5 for more guidance on H-2A visa workers.

**Withholding adjustment for nonresident alien employees (p. 27).** Nonresident aliens may not claim the standard deduction on their tax returns, so for nonresident alien employees performing services within the United States the employer must add an amount to wages when figuring the federal income tax to withhold. The addition is made solely to calculate federal income tax withholding. It appears in no box of the employee's Form W-2 and doesn't raise the employee's income tax liability. Nor does it raise the social security or Medicare tax liability of the employer or the employee, or the employer's FUTA tax liability. For the amount to add to wages for the payroll period, the page refers to Withholding Adjustment for Nonresident Alien Employees under Introduction in Pub. 15-T.

**Supplemental wage payment (p. 27).** The adjustment for nonresident alien employees is not applied to a supplemental wage payment (the page refers to section 7) when the 37% mandatory flat rate withholding applies, or when the 22% optional flat rate withholding is being used to calculate income tax withholding on that payment.

**Nonresident alien employee's Form W-4 (p. 27).** On Form W-4, nonresident aliens are required to:
- Not claim exemption from income tax withholding, even if they meet both of the conditions for exemption listed in the Form W-4 instructions.
- Request withholding as if single, whatever their actual filing status.
- Not claim the child tax credit or credit for other dependents in Step 3. A nonresident alien who is a resident of Canada, Mexico or South Korea, a student from India or a business apprentice from India may claim them under certain circumstances; the page refers to Pub. 519, U.S. Tax Guide for Aliens.
- Write "Nonresident Alien" or "NRA" in the space below Step 4(c).

An employer that maintains an electronic Form W-4 system should provide a field in which nonresident aliens enter their nonresident alien status instead of writing it below Step 4(c). Nonresident aliens should be told to see Notice 1392, Supplemental Form W-4 Instructions for Nonresident Aliens, before completing Form W-4.

**Form 8233 (p. 27).** A nonresident alien employee claiming a tax treaty exemption from withholding must submit Form 8233, rather than Form W-4, for the income exempt under the treaty. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 8233 and to Pay for Personal Services Performed under Withholding on Specific Income in Pub. 515.

**IRS review of requested Forms W-4 (pp. 27–28).** On the IRS's request you must make original Forms W-4 available for inspection by an IRS employee. You may be directed to send certain Forms W-4 to the IRS, and a notice may require you to submit a copy of Form W-4 for one or more named employees; send the copy or copies to the address given, in the manner the notice directs. The IRS may also require you to submit copies of Form W-4 as directed by a revenue procedure or notice published in the Internal Revenue Bulletin. After sending a requested copy, keep withholding federal income tax on that Form W-4 if it is valid (the page refers to Invalid Forms W-4, later in the section). Should the IRS afterwards inform you in writing that the employee may not claim an exemption from withholding, or the amount of deductions or credits claimed, withhold federal income tax on the basis of the effective date, the employee's permitted filing status and the withholding instructions in the IRS notice (commonly referred to as a "lock-in letter").

**Initial lock-in letter (p. 28).** Information reported on Form W-2 is what the IRS uses to identify employees with withholding compliance problems. Where a serious underwithholding problem exists for a particular employee, the IRS may in some cases issue the employer a lock-in letter stating the employee's permitted filing status and giving withholding instructions for that employee. The employer also receives a copy for the employee, which identifies the permitted filing status, describes the withholding instructions the employer must follow, and describes how the employee can give the IRS additional information so that it can determine the appropriate withholding and/or modify the filing status. The employee copy must be furnished to an employee employed by you on the notice date within 10 business days of receipt, and any reasonable business practice may be used to furnish it. Begin withholding on the basis of the notice on the date the notice specifies.

**Implementation of lock-in letter (p. 28).** On receiving the notice, you may not start withholding on its basis immediately. You must begin withholding on the basis of the notice for any wages paid after the date the notice specifies. The gap between receiving the notice and that date gives the employee time to contact the IRS.

**Seasonal employees and employees not currently performing services (p. 28).** A notice for an employee who isn't currently performing services for you still requires you to furnish the employee copy and to withhold on the basis of the notice if any of these apply:
- Wages you are paying for the employee's prior services are subject to income tax withholding on or after the date specified in the notice.
- Within 12 months of the date of the notice, you reasonably expect the employee to resume services.
- The employee is on a leave of absence not exceeding 12 months, or has a right to reemployment after the leave.

**Termination and rehire of employees (p. 28).** Where you must furnish and withhold on the basis of the notice and the employment relationship ends after the notice date, you must keep withholding on that basis for as long as you continue to pay any wages subject to income tax withholding. You must also withhold on the basis of the notice or the modification notice (explained next) if the employee resumes the employment relationship with you within 12 months after it ended.

**Modification notice (p. 28).** After the original notice, the IRS may issue a later notice (a modification notice) changing it. The modification notice may alter the permitted filing status and the withholding instructions, and you must withhold federal income tax on the basis of the effective date it specifies.

**New Form W-4 after IRS notice (p. 28).** After a notice or modification notice, disregard a new Form W-4 from the employee that claims complete exemption from withholding or that results in less withholding than the notice or modification notice would. Withhold on the basis of the notice or modification notice unless the IRS tells you to withhold on the basis of the new Form W-4. An employee who wants a new Form W-4 resulting in less withholding than required to be put into effect must contact the IRS. If, though, the employee gives you a new completed Form W-4 resulting in more withholding than the notice or modification notice would, withhold on the basis of the new Form W-4. Disregard any other later Forms W-4 from the employee and withhold on the basis of the IRS notice or modification notice.

**Caution: lock-in letters from before 2020 (p. 28).** A lock-in letter received for an employee in a year before 2020 should still be followed for 2026. The withholding methods in Pub. 15-T for an employee with a Form W-4 from 2019 or earlier apply, or you may use the optional computational bridge for treating 2019 and earlier Forms W-4 as 2020 or later Forms W-4 when figuring federal income tax withholding; the page refers to How To Treat 2019 and Earlier Forms W-4 as if They Were 2020 or Later Forms W-4 under Introduction in Pub. 15-T. Keep following the pre-2020 lock-in letter until you receive a letter releasing the employee from the lock-in procedures, you receive a modification notice, or the employee gives you a new Form W-4 resulting in more withholding than the notice would. The page points to IRS.gov/WHC for additional information about employer withholding compliance.

**Substitute Forms W-4 (p. 28).** Employees are encouraged to use the official Form W-4, though you may use a substitute version to meet your business needs. A substitute must contain language identical to the official Form W-4 and meet all current IRS rules for substitute forms. When you give the employee your substitute form, you must also provide all tables, instructions and worksheets from the current Form W-4. The page refers to Pub. 15-T for more information. You can't accept substitute Forms W-4 that employees developed. An employee who submits an employee-developed substitute after October 10, 2007, is treated as failing to furnish a Form W-4, but valid employee-developed Forms W-4 you accepted before October 11, 2007, are still to be honored.

**Invalid Forms W-4 (pp. 28–29).** An unauthorized change or addition to Form W-4 makes it invalid, and that includes removing any language by which the employee certifies that the form is correct. A Form W-4 is also invalid if, by the date the employee gives it to you, the employee clearly indicates it is false. An employee submitting a false Form W-4 may be subject to a $500 penalty. You may treat a Form W-4 as invalid when the employee has written "exempt" below Step 4(c) and has also checked the box in Step 2(c) or entered numbers for Steps 3 and 4. An invalid Form W-4 must not be used to figure federal income tax withholding; tell the employee it is invalid and ask for another one. If no valid one is given and you hold an earlier valid Form W-4 for the employee, withhold as before. Without an earlier valid Form W-4, withhold as though the employee had checked the box for Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c) and made no entries in Steps 2, 3 or 4 of the 2026 Form W-4. An employee who was paid wages in 2019, never submitted a valid Form W-4 and submits an invalid one in 2026 continues, however, to be treated as single and claiming zero allowances on a 2019 Form W-4. With the optional computational bridge, described earlier under Redesigned Form W-4, you may treat this employee as having checked the box for Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c), made no entries in Steps 2 and 3, entered $8,600 in Step 4(a) and entered zero in Step 4(b) of the 2026 Form W-4.

**Amounts exempt from levy on wages, salary and other income (p. 29).** On receiving a Notice of Levy on Wages, Salary, and Other Income (a notice in the Form 668 series), you must withhold amounts as the instructions for these forms describe. Pub. 1494 has tables for figuring the amount exempt from levy. Where a levy issued in a prior year is still in effect and the taxpayer submits a new Statement of Exemptions and Filing Status, the current year Pub. 1494 is used to figure the exempt amount.

## Note
**Eligibility for employment (p. 7).** The legal eligibility of every new employee to work in the United States (American Samoa, Guam, the CNMI, the USVI and Puerto Rico included) must be verified. That involves completing U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) Form I-9, Employment Eligibility Verification, which is available at USCIS.gov/Forms. For more information the page points to USCIS.gov/I-9-Central, or to 800-375-5283 or 800-767-1833 (TTY).

**Verifying a name and an SSN (p. 7).** The Social Security Number Verification Service (SSNVS), at SSA.gov/employer/ssnv.htm, may be used to check that an employee name matches a social security number (SSN). A person can hold a valid SSN without being authorized to work in the United States. E-Verify, at E-Verify.gov, may be used to confirm the employment eligibility of newly hired employees.

**New hire reporting (p. 7).** All 50 states and most of the territories have a new hire registry, and each new employee must be reported to a designated state new hire registry. A new employee is someone not previously employed by you, or previously employed by you but separated from that employment for at least 60 consecutive days. Many states accept a copy of Form W-4 with employer information added, and the page points to acf.gov/css/employers for more. Employers in American Samoa, Guam, the CNMI, the USVI and Puerto Rico should contact their local government about their new hire registry.

**W-4 request (p. 7).** Ask each new employee to complete the 2026 Form W-4. The page refers to section 9.

**Name and SSN (p. 7).** Record each new employee’s name and SSN as shown on the social security card, if it is available. Where the employee can’t provide the card, you should verify the SSN and the employee’s eligibility for employment as discussed under Verification of SSNs in section 4. An employee with no social security card should apply for one, and the page refers to section 4.

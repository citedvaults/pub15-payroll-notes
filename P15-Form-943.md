## Note
- Return transcripts for Forms 940, 941, 943, 944 and 945 for tax years 2023 and later are now available through your IRS business tax account (F03, p. 3).
- The COVID-19 related sick and family leave wage credit applies only to leave taken after March 31, 2020, and before October 1, 2021, and Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 can’t any longer be used to claim it (F04, p. 3).
- For tax periods beginning after 2023 the claim lines were removed from Form 943, Employer’s Annual Tax Return for Agricultural Employees, and from Forms 941 and 944. An employer entitled to the credit for wages paid after 2023 for an earlier applicable leave period claims it on Form 943-X (or Form 941-X or Form 944-X) (F04, pp. 3–4).
- A Form 943-X filed before the Form 943 for the year may cause errors or processing delays (F04, p. 4).
- The amount from Form 8974, line 12 or, if applicable, line 17, is reported on Form 943 (or Form 941 or Form 944); for reducing your record of tax liability for the payroll tax credit, the page points to the line 17 instructions in the Instructions for Form 943 (F04, p. 4).
- Approved section 3504 agents and CPEOs must complete Schedule R (Form 943), Allocation Schedule for Aggregate Form 943 Filers, for an aggregate Form 943 (F04, p. 4).
- An aggregate annual Form 943 is filed by an agent the IRS approved under section 3504, and also by CPEOs approved under section 7705 (F04, p. 4).
- CPEOs generally must file Form 941 or Form 943, with the applicable Schedule R, electronically (F04, p. 5).
- Other third-party payers filing an aggregate quarterly Form 941 or annual Form 943, non-certified PEOs for example, file the applicable Schedule R when their clients claim an employment tax credit (F04, p. 5).
- A mistake on a filed Form 943 is corrected with Form 943-X, which is filed on its own and separately from Form 943 (F04, p. 5).
- Unless you have filed a “final” Form 940 and a “final” Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, you continue to file Form 943 for periods with no wages paid (the “seasonal” employer condition is for Form 941 only); the IRS encourages filing “zero wage” returns electronically (F04, p. 6).
- The Employer Responsibilities list includes filing Form 943 if required, with payment of the tax with the return when no deposit is required, among the items under Annually (see Calendar for due dates) (F09, p. 8).
- Payroll withholding (Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944) and nonpayroll withholding (Form 945) are deposited separately (F10, p. 8).
- Income tax withheld on distributions to participants from some nonqualified pension and deferred compensation plans goes on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, not on Form 945; distributions from such plans to a beneficiary or estate of a deceased employee aren't wages, and the income tax withheld on them goes on Form 945 (F10, pp. 8–9).
- The filing address for Form 943 generally turns on where your residence or principal place of business is located and on whether a payment goes with the return; tax-exempt organizations and government entities have separate addresses, found in the separate instructions for Form 943 (F14, pp. 9–10).
- For depositing taxes reported on Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 and Form 945, the Calendar refers to section 11 (F21, p. 10).
- Under the Calendar’s By January 31 date, agricultural employers file Form 943 for the previous calendar year and deposit any income, social security and Medicare taxes not yet deposited (F21, p. 11).
- Those taxes may be paid with Form 943 when the year’s total tax liability (Form 943, line 13) is under $2,500, and timely depositors of all taxes may file by February 10 (F21, p. 11).
- If an EIN you used on Form 943 (or Form 941 or Form 944) differs from the EIN reported on Form W-3, the page refers to Box h—Other EIN used this year in the General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 (S01, p. 13).
- The deposit schedule depends on the total tax liability reported on Form 943, line 13 (and on the other forms the page lists) during a lookback period (S11.01, p. 32).
- For a Form 943 filer in the current year or either of the 2 years before, the schedule comes from the taxes reported in the second preceding calendar year; for 2026 the lookback period for a Form 943 or Form 944 filer is calendar year 2024 (S11.01, p. 32).
- A Form 943 filer whose lookback taxes (Form 943, line 13) were $50,000 or less is a monthly schedule depositor for the calendar year, and one whose taxes were more than $50,000 is a semiweekly schedule depositor (S11.01.01, p. 33; S11.01.02, p. 33).
- Monthly schedule depositors shouldn’t file Form 943 monthly (S11.01.01, p. 33).
- For Form 943 filers, liability from before the business began or was acquired counts as zero in the lookback period, so a new filer is a monthly schedule depositor in its first and second calendar years; however, the page points to the $100,000 Next-Day Deposit Rule (S11.01.01, p. 33).
- A semiweekly schedule depositor completes Form 943-A, Agricultural Employer’s Record of Federal Tax Liability, and submits it with Form 943 (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- For annual Form 943 the return period is a calendar year (S11.01.02, p. 33).
- Red Co., in the example for employers of farmworkers, reported $48,000 of taxes on its 2024 Form 943, line 13, and $60,000 on its 2025 Form 943, line 13; it is a monthly schedule depositor for 2026 because its lookback taxes ($48,000 for calendar year 2024) were not over $50,000 (S11.01.03, p. 34).
- When you deposited too much, you choose on Form 943 for that year whether the overpayment is refunded or credited to your next return (S11.02, p. 36).
- The deposit penalty information is written about Form 941 but applies to Form 943 as well (S11.03, p. 36).
- To avoid an “averaged” FTD penalty, a monthly schedule depositor lists tax liabilities (not deposits) in the monthly entry spaces on Form 943, line 17, and a semiweekly schedule depositor lists them on Form 943-A; check that the total liability on Form 943, line 17, or Form 943-A, line M, equals the liability on Form 943, line 13 (S11.03, p. 37).
- Form 943 is due for each calendar year starting with the first year in which you pay $2,500 or more for farmwork or employ a farmworker who meets the $150 test explained in section 9; these wages are not reported on Form 941, Form 944 or Form 945 (S12, p. 38).
- Your 2025 Form 943 is due by February 2, 2026, or by February 10, 2026 if timely deposits fully paid the year’s taxes (S12, p. 38).
- An employer that files Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 for business employees may add the taxes of household employees to that return, and agricultural employers report their social security, Medicare and withheld income taxes on Form 943 (S12, p. 38).
- Form 943 can be filed through the IRS e-file program, and reporting agents filing it for groups of taxpayers can file electronically (S12, p. 38).
- CPEOs, apart from the first quarter (Form 941 only) for which a CPEO is certified, must file Form 941 with Schedule R (Form 941) electronically, or where applicable Form 943 with Schedule R (Form 943); the IRS may waive electronic filing in certain circumstances (S12, pp. 38–39).
- An agricultural employer files only one Form 943 per year (S12, p. 39).
- Name and EIN on Form 943 should read exactly as on earlier returns, and the Instructions for Form 943 explain how to prepare the form (S12, p. 39).
- A business that closes files a final return for the last year in which wages are paid (S12, p. 39).
- A late Form 943 for a previous year is filed on a copy of the form (with separate instructions) with a revision date showing that year; the page gives IRS.gov/Form943 for prior year forms (S12, p. 39).
- Social security and Medicare wages and taxes are reported separately on Forms W-2 and W-3 and on Forms 941, 943 or 944, and Form W-3 is reconciled with the annual Form 943 (S12, p. 40).
- Only taxes and withholding that appear on Form W-2 belong on Forms 941, 943 or 944 (S12, p. 40).
- Where Additional Medicare Tax was withheld, the Medicare tax on Form 943, line 5, will not be double the Medicare tax withheld in box 6 of Form W-3, because that tax falls on the employee alone (S12, p. 40).
- Certain social security and Medicare amounts reported on Form 943, lines 3, 5 and 7, have to be adjusted to reach the correct tax liability, and current period adjustments are reported on Form 943, line 10 (S13, p. 40).
- Fractions-of-cents adjustments go on Form 943, line 10, and an employer paying with Form 943 rather than depositing, because the year’s total taxes are under $2,500, may also report one (S13.01, pp. 40–41).
- Under the adjustment of tax on third-party sick pay, the employer share and the employee share of social security and Medicare taxes on sick pay are reported on Form 943, lines 2 and 4; the Additional Medicare Tax goes on line 7 when the employer’s and third-party payer’s combined wages for an employee exceed $200,000 for the calendar year; and the taxes a third-party payer withheld on sick pay are a negative adjustment on line 10 (S13.01, p. 41).
- For group-term life insurance premiums over $50,000 paid for a former employee, Form 943 filers put the social security wages and Medicare wages on lines 2 and 4 and the taxes on lines 3 and 5; if the premium amount combined with other wages exceeds $200,000 for the calendar year, the Additional Medicare Tax wages go on line 6 and the tax on line 7, and the employee share is backed out as a negative adjustment on line 10 (S13.01, p. 41).
- The supporting statement for these adjustments is not attached to Form 943 (S13.01, p. 41).
- For current period adjustments you make no change to your record of federal tax liability on Form 943, line 17, or Form 943-A (S13.01, p. 41).
- An error found on a filed Form 943 is corrected on a separate Form 943-X for each Form 943 corrected, and Form 943-X is filed on its own, not with Form 943 (S13.02, p. 42).
- Treasury Decision 9405 changed how interest-free adjustments to employment taxes reported on Form 943 are made and how a refund claim is filed (S13.02, p. 42).
- Income tax withheld in a prior calendar year can’t be adjusted unless the adjustment corrects an administrative error or section 3509 applies; an administrative error is an amount entered on Form 941, 943 or 944 that differs from the amount actually withheld (S13.02, p. 42).
- Where you did not repay or reimburse the employee, you report and pay the excess when you file the Form 943 for the year in which too much tax was withheld (S13.02, p. 43).
- FUTA tax for household employees need not be deposited unless their wages are reported on Form 941, 943 or 944; where they weren’t reported there, FUTA tax for them goes on Schedule H (Form 1040) (S14, pp. 44–45).
- A CPEO completes Schedule R (Form 943) when it files an aggregate Form 943 (S16, p. 54).
- Return transcripts for Form 943 for tax years 2023 and later can be accessed through your IRS business tax account (B04, p. 57).

## Note
The penalty information is written about Form 941 but applies equally to Form 943, Form 944 and Form 945. It does not apply when the employer qualifies for the exceptions discussed under Payment with return, earlier in section 11 (p. 36).

**When penalties apply (p. 36).** A penalty may apply to deposits that are late or for less than the amount required. None applies when the failure to deposit properly and on time was due to reasonable cause and not to willful neglect, and a taxpayer who receives a penalty notice can explain why reasonable cause exists.

**Waivers (p. 36).** For a timely filed employment tax return, the IRS may also waive deposit penalties where the failure was inadvertent and either it was the first quarter in which any employment tax deposit was required, or it was the first deposit after the deposit frequency changed. The net worth and size limitations that apply to awards of administrative and litigation costs under section 7430 must also be met: an individual’s net worth can’t exceed $2 million, and a business’s net worth can’t exceed $7 million with no more than 500 employees. The IRS may also waive the penalty for the first time a deposit is required if the payment was inadvertently sent to the IRS instead of being deposited by EFT.

**Penalty rates (p. 36).** For amounts not properly or timely deposited:
- 2% for deposits made 1 to 5 days late.
- 5% for deposits made 6 to 15 days late.
- 10% for deposits made at least 16 days late, provided they are made before 10 days have passed from the date of the IRS’s first notice asking for the tax due.
- 10% for amounts that should have been deposited but were instead paid directly to the IRS or paid with the tax return; the page points to Payment with return, earlier in section 11, for exceptions.
- 15% for amounts still unpaid more than 10 days after the date of the first IRS notice asking for the tax due, or the day on which notice and demand for immediate payment was received, whichever is earlier.

Late deposit penalty amounts are worked out in calendar days, counted from the due date of the liability (p. 36).

**Former Form 944 filers (p. 36).** Where Form 944 was filed for the prior year and Forms 941 are filed for the current year, the FTD penalty doesn’t apply to a late deposit of the current year’s January employment taxes, provided the taxes are deposited in full by March 15 of that year.

**Order in which deposits are applied (p. 36).** Deposits generally go against the most recent tax liability within the quarter. After an FTD penalty notice, you may designate how your deposits are applied so as to minimize the penalty, provided you do so within 90 days of the notice’s date and follow the instructions on the notice; the page refers to Revenue Procedure 2001-58 for more on designating deposits. Example: Cedar, Inc., owes deposits of $1,000 on April 15 and $1,500 on May 15, skips the April 15 deposit and deposits $2,000 on May 15. Applying deposits to the most recent liability puts $1,500 against the May 15 deposit and the remaining $500 against April, which leaves $500 of the April 15 liability undeposited, and the penalty on that underdeposit applies as explained earlier.

**Trust fund recovery penalty (pp. 36–37).** The trust fund recovery penalty may apply when federal income, social security or Medicare tax that must be withheld (trust fund taxes) is not withheld, or is not deposited or paid to the U.S. Treasury. It equals 100% of the unpaid trust fund tax. When those taxes can’t be collected immediately from the employer or business, the penalty may be imposed on every person the IRS determines to be responsible for collecting, accounting for or paying them over and who acted willfully in not doing so. It doesn’t apply to trust fund taxes an employer holds back in anticipation of credits it is entitled to.

A responsible person can be any of the following: an officer or employee of a corporation; a partner or employee of a partnership; an accountant; a volunteer director or trustee; an employee of a sole proprietorship; or another person or entity responsible for collecting, accounting for or paying over trust fund taxes. The term may also cover someone who signs the business’s checks or otherwise has authority to cause business funds to be spent (pp. 36–37).

Willfully means voluntarily, consciously and intentionally. A responsible person acts willfully by knowing that the collecting, accounting for or paying over of trust fund taxes isn’t happening, or by recklessly disregarding obvious and known risks to the government’s right to receive them (p. 37).

**Separate accounting (p. 37).** Separate accounting may be required where withheld employee social security, Medicare or income tax isn’t paid over, or required taxes aren’t deposited, required payments aren’t made, or tax returns aren’t filed. The IRS would then give written notice requiring deposits into a special trust account for the U.S. Government, and failing to meet the special bank deposit requirements for that account may bring criminal penalties.

**“Averaged” FTD penalty (p. 37).** The page pairs each return’s tax liability line with the line on which liabilities are reported: Form 941, liability on line 12, reported on line 16 (or on Schedule B (Form 941)); Form 943, liability on line 13, reported on line 17 (or on Form 943-A); Form 944, liability on line 9, reported on line 13 (or on Form 945-A); Form 945, liability on line 3, reported on line 7 (or on Form 945-A). The IRS may assess an “averaged” FTD penalty of 2% to 10% in two cases:
- A monthly schedule depositor didn’t properly complete the reporting line (16, 17, 13 or 7) when the liability line (12, 13, 9 or 3) equaled or exceeded $2,500.
- A semiweekly schedule depositor’s liability on that line equaled or exceeded $2,500 and the depositor completed the reporting line (Form 941 line 16, Form 943 line 17, Form 944 line 13 or Form 945 line 7) in place of Schedule B (Form 941), Form 943-A or Form 945-A (Form 945-A for Forms 944 and 945), failed to attach a properly completed Schedule B (Form 941), Form 943-A or Form 945-A as applicable, or completed that schedule improperly, for example by entering tax deposits instead of tax liabilities in the numbered spaces.

The penalty is figured by spreading the total tax liability from the liability line evenly across the tax period. Deposits and payments are then applied to those averaged liabilities in the date order the IRS received them, and the penalty is figured on any tax not deposited, deposited late or not deposited in the correct amounts. Deposits and payments may not be counted as timely, because the actual dates of the liabilities can’t be accurately determined.

**Avoiding an “averaged” FTD penalty (p. 37).** The penalty can be avoided by reviewing the return before filing it. Before submitting Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 or Form 945, take these steps:
- A monthly schedule depositor reports tax liabilities (not deposits) in the monthly entry spaces of the reporting line (Form 941 line 16, Form 943 line 17, Form 944 line 13 or Form 945 line 7).
- A semiweekly schedule depositor reports tax liabilities (not deposits) on Schedule B (Form 941), Form 943-A or Form 945-A, as applicable, on the lines for the dates employees were paid.
- Check that the total liability on the reporting line, or at the bottom of Schedule B (Form 941) or on line M of Form 943-A or Form 945-A, equals the liability on the liability line: Form 941 line 16 (or the bottom of Schedule B) against line 12; Form 943 line 17 (or Form 943-A line M) against line 13; Form 944 line 13 (or Form 945-A line M) against line 9; Form 945 line 7 (or Form 945-A line M) against line 3.
- Don’t show negative amounts on the reporting lines or on Schedule B (Form 941), Form 943-A or Form 945-A.
- For prior-period errors, don’t adjust the liabilities reported on those lines or schedules; file an adjusted return (Form 941-X, 943-X, 944-X or 945-X) if you are also adjusting your tax liability. If you are only adjusting deposits in response to an FTD penalty notice, the page refers to the Instructions for Schedule B (Form 941), for Form 943-A (for Form 943) or for Form 945-A (for Forms 944 and 945).

**Criminal prosecution (p. 37).** On top of civil penalties, you may face criminal prosecution (a trial) for willful acts of these kinds: evading tax; failing to collect tax, or to account for and pay it over truthfully; failing to file a return, supply information or pay tax due; furnishing false or fraudulent Forms W-2 to employees, or not furnishing Forms W-2; committing fraud and giving false statements; preparing and filing a fraudulent return; and identity theft.

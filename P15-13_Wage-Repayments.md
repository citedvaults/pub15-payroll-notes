## Note
If an employee repays wages that were received in error, the repayment is not offset against current year wages unless the amounts were received in error in the current year (p. 43).

**Repayment of current-year wages (p. 43).** When the employer receives repayments of wages paid in a prior quarter of the current year, it reports adjustments on Form 941-X to recover the income tax withholding and the social security and Medicare taxes on the repaid wages.

**Repayment of prior-year wages (p. 43).** When the employer receives repayments of wages paid in a prior year, it reports an adjustment on Form 941-X, Form 943-X or Form 944-X to recover the social security and Medicare taxes. No adjustment can be made for income tax withholding, because the wages were income to the employee for the prior year. No adjustment can be made for Additional Medicare Tax withholding, because the employee determines liability for Additional Medicare Tax on their own income tax return for the prior year. The employer must also file Forms W-2c and W-3c with the SSA to correct social security and Medicare wages and taxes. Wages (box 1) on Form W-2c are not corrected for the amount paid in error. A copy of Form W-2c goes to the employee.

**Employee reporting of repayment (p. 43).** Wages paid in error in the prior year stay taxable to the employee for that year, since the employee received and had use of the funds then. The employee is not entitled to file an amended return (Form 1040-X) to get back the income tax on those wages. A deduction or credit for the repaid wages may be available instead, on the employee's income tax return for the year of repayment. Additional Medicare Tax paid on the wages paid in error in the prior year is treated differently: the employee should file an amended return (Form 1040-X) to recover it. An employee who asks how to report a wage repayment may be pointed by the employer to Repayments in Pub. 525, Taxable and Nontaxable Income.

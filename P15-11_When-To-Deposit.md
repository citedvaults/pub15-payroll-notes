## Note
Two deposit schedules, monthly and semiweekly, set when a deposit is due once a tax liability arises, for social security, Medicare and withheld federal income taxes. The liability depends on the dates payments were made or wages were paid. For taxable noncash fringe benefits, the page points to the text in section 5 on when taxable fringe benefits are treated as paid.

Before each calendar year begins, you must work out which of the two schedules you are required to use. The choice rests on the total tax liability you reported during a lookback period on Form 941 (line 12), Form 943 (line 13), Form 944 (line 9) or Form 945 (line 3). How often you pay employees or make deposits does not decide it. Later in the section the page points to special rules for Forms 943, 944 and 945, and to Application of Monthly and Semiweekly Schedules.

**Caution.** FUTA tax falls outside these rules; section 14 covers depositing it.

**Lookback period.** For a Form 941 filer the lookback period is 4 quarters, running from July 1 to June 30 as Table 1 shows, and the total taxes reported on line 12 of Forms 941 for it fix the schedule for the calendar year. Reporting $50,000 or less of taxes for the lookback period makes you a monthly schedule depositor; reporting more than $50,000 makes you a semiweekly schedule depositor.

Table 1. Lookback Period for Calendar Year 2026* (p. 32), its four columns:

| Lookback quarter 1 | Lookback quarter 2 | Lookback quarter 3 | Lookback quarter 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| July 1, 2024, through Sept. 30, 2024 | Oct. 1, 2024, through Dec. 31, 2024 | Jan. 1, 2025, through Mar. 31, 2025 | Apr. 1, 2025, through June 30, 2025 |
| * The lookback period for a 2026 Form 941 filer who filed Form 944 in either 2024 or 2025 is calendar year 2024. |  |  |  |

A filer of Form 943 or Form 944 for the current year or for either of the 2 years before it looks to the total taxes reported in the second preceding calendar year, whether on Forms 941 for all 4 quarters of that year, on that year's Form 943 or on that year's Form 944. For 2026 that lookback period is calendar year 2024 for a Form 943 or Form 944 filer. Reporting $50,000 or less of taxes for it makes you a monthly schedule depositor, and reporting more than $50,000 makes you a semiweekly schedule depositor.

A Form 945 filer uses the total taxes reported on line 3 of its Form 945 for the second preceding calendar year; for 2026 that is calendar year 2024.

**Adjustments and the lookback rule.** For the lookback rule, the tax liability of earlier periods is not changed by adjustments made on Form 941-X, Form 943-X, Form 944-X or Form 945-X.

**Example.** An employer first reported a tax liability of $45,000 for the lookback period. In January 2026 it found that the tax for one lookback quarter had been understated by $10,000, and it fixed the error with Form 941-X. It is still a monthly schedule depositor for 2026, since lookback liabilities rest on the amounts first reported, and those were $50,000 or less. The $10,000 adjustment is also not counted as part of the 2026 taxes.

**Deposit period.** A deposit period is the span over which tax liabilities build up for each required deposit due date. For a monthly schedule depositor it is a calendar month; for a semiweekly schedule depositor the deposit periods run Wednesday through Friday and Saturday through Tuesday.

**Tip.** An agent with an approved Form 2678 applies the deposit rules to the total employment taxes it accumulates for its own employees and on behalf of every employer it is authorized to act for. The page refers to Revenue Procedure 2013-39 for more on such an agent.

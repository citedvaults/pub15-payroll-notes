## Note
**Electronic filing of amended employment tax returns (p. 3).** An amended Form 940, Form 941-X, Form 943-X or Form 945-X can be filed electronically through Modernized e-File (MeF). For now MeF cannot be used for Form 944-X or Form CT-1 X. The page points to IRS.gov/EmploymentEfile for more on electronic filing.

**Pub. 15 is for all employers (p. 3).** All employers use Pub. 15, agricultural employers and employers in the U.S. territories among them. Pub. 51, Pub. 80 and Pub. 179 have been discontinued. If you prefer a Spanish version of Pub. 15, the page refers to Pub. 15 (sp).

**Form references (p. 3).** Unless otherwise noted, the publication’s references to Form W-2 cover Forms W-2AS, W-2CM, W-2GU and W-2VI and Form 499R-2/W-2PR. References to Form W-2c cover Form 499R-2c/W-2cPR, those to Form W-3 cover Form W-3SS and Form W-3 (PR), and those to Form W-3c cover Form W-3C (PR).

**COVID-19 related credit for qualified sick and family leave wages (pp. 3–4).** This credit applies only to leave taken after March 31, 2020, and before October 1, 2021, and can no longer be claimed on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944. Generally, two credits have expired. The first, under the Families First Coronavirus Response Act (as amended and extended by the COVID-related Tax Relief Act of 2020), covered leave taken after March 31, 2020, and before April 1, 2021. The second, under sections 3131, 3132 and 3133 of the Internal Revenue Code (enacted under the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021), covered leave taken after March 31, 2021, and before October 1, 2021. Even so, employers that in 2024, 2025 or 2026 pay qualified sick and family leave wages for leave taken after March 31, 2020, and before October 1, 2021, are eligible to claim a credit for those wages in 2024, 2025 or 2026. For tax periods beginning after 2023 the lines for claiming the credit are gone from Form 941, Form 943 and Form 944, because it would be extremely rare for an employer to pay such wages after 2023 for leave taken in that earlier period. An employer eligible because it paid the wages after 2023 for an earlier applicable leave period claims the credit for the wages paid that year by filing Form 941-X, Form 943-X or Form 944-X. Filing one of those before the Form 941 for the quarter, or the Form 943 or Form 944 for the year, may lead to errors or delays in processing it.

**Form 941 (sp), Form 943 (sp) and Form 944 (sp) (p. 4).** Form 941 (sp), Form 943 (sp) and Form 944 (sp) can be filed by anyone who prefers the form and its instructions in Spanish.

**Qualified small business payroll tax credit for increasing research activities (p. 4).** For tax years beginning before 2023, a qualified small business may elect to treat up to $250,000 of its credit for increasing research activities as a payroll tax credit. For tax years beginning after 2022 the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (the IRA) raises that election amount to $500,000. The election must be made on or before the due date of the originally filed income tax return (including extensions). The part of the credit used against payroll taxes is allowed in the first calendar quarter that begins after the qualified small business filed its income tax return. Form 6765 is where the election and the determination of the credit amount to use against the employer’s payroll taxes are made, and that amount must then be reported on Form 8974. From the first quarter of 2023, the credit first reduces the employer share of social security tax, by up to $250,000 per quarter. Any credit remaining then reduces the employer share of Medicare tax for the quarter, and whatever is left after both reductions carries forward to the next quarter. Form 8974 determines how much of the credit can be used in the current quarter, and its line 12 amount (or, if applicable, its line 17 amount) is reported on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944. The page points to IRS.gov/ResearchPayrollTC, and to the line 16 instructions in the Instructions for Form 941 (line 17 instructions in those for Form 943, line 13 instructions in those for Form 944) on reducing your record of tax liability for this credit.

**Disaster tax relief (p. 4).** Relief is available for those affected by disasters, and the page points to IRS.gov/DisasterTaxRelief.

**2026 withholding tables (p. 4).** Pub. 15-T, available at IRS.gov/Pub15T, holds the Percentage Method and Wage Bracket Method withholding tables, the employer instructions on figuring employee withholding, and the amount to add to a nonresident alien employee’s wages when figuring federal income tax withholding.

**Certification program for professional employer organizations (PEOs) (p. 4).** The Stephen Beck, Jr., Achieving a Better Life Experience (ABLE) Act of 2014 required the IRS to set up a voluntary certification program for PEOs. PEOs take on various payroll administration and tax reporting duties for business clients, and are typically paid a fee based on payroll costs. To become and remain certified, a certified professional employer organization (CPEO) must meet various requirements, which sections 3511 and 7705 and related published guidance describe. A CPEO’s certification may affect the employment tax liabilities of the CPEO and of its customers. For employment tax purposes a CPEO is generally treated as the employer of any individual who performs services for a customer of the CPEO under a contract between the CPEO and the customer that is described in section 7705(e)(2) (a CPEO contract). That treatment reaches only the wages and other compensation the CPEO itself pays the individual. An organization must apply through the IRS Online Registration System to become a CPEO; the page points to IRS.gov/CPEO and to Revenue Procedure 2023-18, 2023-13 I.R.B. 605.

**Outsourcing payroll duties (p. 4).** As an employer you are generally responsible for seeing that returns are filed and deposits and payments are made, even when you contract with a third party to do these things, and you stay responsible if the third party fails to perform a required action. Before you outsource payroll and related tax duties to a third-party payer (a payroll service provider or reporting agent, for instance), meaning withholding, reporting and paying over social security, Medicare, FUTA and federal income taxes, the page points to IRS.gov/OutsourcingPayrollDuties for helpful information. Where a CPEO pays wages and other compensation to an individual who performs services for you under a CPEO contract, the CPEO is generally treated as the employer, but only for what the CPEO pays that individual. For certain employees covered by a CPEO contract, though, you may also be treated as their employer and may therefore be liable for federal employment taxes on the wages and other compensation the CPEO pays them. The page refers to section 16 for the different types of third-party payer arrangements.

**Aggregate Form 941 or Form 943 filers (pp. 4–5).** Approved section 3504 agents and CPEOs filing an aggregate Form 941 or Form 943 must complete the applicable Schedule R: Schedule R (Form 941), Allocation Schedule for Aggregate Form 941 Filers, or Schedule R (Form 943), Allocation Schedule for Aggregate Form 943 Filers. The page names these filers of an aggregate quarterly Form 941 or annual Form 943:
- An agent the IRS has approved under section 3504 of the Internal Revenue Code. An agent seeking approval to act for an employer files Form 2678 with the IRS, unless you are a state or local government agency acting as an agent under the special procedures in Revenue Procedure 2013-39, 2013-52 I.R.B. 830.
- Also a CPEO approved by the IRS under section 7705; an organization applies through the IRS Online Registration System at IRS.gov/CPEO to become one. CPEOs file Form 8973 to tell the IRS that they have started or ended a service contract with a client or customer. A CPEO must generally file Form 941 or Form 943, and the applicable Schedule R, electronically, and for that requirement the page refers to Revenue Procedure 2023-18.

Another third-party payer that files an aggregate quarterly Form 941 or annual Form 943, such as a non-certified PEO, must complete and file the applicable Schedule R if its clients are claiming any employment tax credit; the qualified small business payroll tax credit for increasing research activities is one example.

**Aggregate Form 940 filers (p. 5).** For an aggregate Form 940, approved section 3504 agents and CPEOs must complete Schedule R (Form 940), Allocation Schedule for Aggregate Form 940 Filers. Agents acting for recipients of home care services provided through a program run by a federal, state or local government can file aggregate Forms 940. To request approval to act as such an agent, the agent files Form 2678 with the IRS, unless you are a state or local government agency acting under the special procedures in Revenue Procedure 2013-39. CPEOs approved by the IRS under section 7705 file aggregate Forms 940 as well, and file Form 8973 to notify the IRS that a service contract with a client or customer has started or ended. They must generally file Form 940 and Schedule R (Form 940) electronically, and the page refers to Revenue Procedure 2023-18 for that requirement.

**Work opportunity tax credit for qualified tax-exempt organizations hiring qualified veterans (p. 5).** A qualified tax-exempt organization that hires eligible unemployed veterans may be able to claim the work opportunity tax credit against its payroll tax liability, using Form 5884-C. The page points to IRS.gov/WOTC.

**Medicaid waiver payments (p. 5).** Under Notice 2014-7, certain Medicaid waiver payments are excludable from income for federal income tax purposes. The page points to Notice 2014-7, 2014-4 I.R.B. 445, and to IRS.gov/MedicaidWaiverPayments for more, including questions and answers.

**Disability payments for injuries from a terrorist attack (p. 5).** A disability payment for injuries incurred as a direct result of a terrorist attack directed against the United States (or its allies) is not included in income. Federal income tax withholding is only required when a payment is includible in income, so none should be withheld from these payments. The page refers to Pub. 907 and Pub. 3920.

**Voluntary withholding on dividends and other distributions by an Alaska Native Corporation (ANC) (p. 5).** A shareholder of an ANC may ask the ANC for voluntary federal income tax withholding on the dividends and other distributions it pays, by giving it a completed Form W-4V. The page refers to Notice 2013-77, 2013-50 I.R.B. 632.

**Definition of marriage (p. 5).** For federal tax purposes:
- a marriage is recognized if the state or territory of the United States where it was entered into recognizes it, regardless of legal residence;
- a relationship that is a marriage under the laws of a foreign jurisdiction or an American Indian tribe is recognized as one if at least one state or territory of the United States would recognize it as a marriage, regardless of legal residence;
- individuals in a registered domestic partnership, civil union or similar relationship that the law of the state or territory where it was entered into does not call a marriage are not lawfully married, regardless of legal residence.

**Differential wage payments (p. 5).** Qualified differential wage payments made by employers to individuals serving in the U.S. Armed Forces carry federal income tax withholding, but not social security, Medicare or FUTA tax. The page refers to section 5.

**Severance payments (p. 5).** Severance payments are wages, so social security and Medicare taxes, federal income tax withholding and FUTA tax apply to them.

**You must receive written notice from the IRS to file Form 944 (p. 5).** An employer that has been filing quarterly Forms 941, believes its employment taxes for the calendar year will be $1,000 or less and would like to file an annual Form 944 instead must contact the IRS during the first calendar quarter of the tax year to ask for it. Written notice from the IRS to file Form 944 in place of quarterly Forms 941 is required before you may file Form 944. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 944 for the methods and deadlines for making a request.

**Employers can request to file quarterly Forms 941 instead of an annual Form 944 (p. 5).** An employer that has received IRS notice to file Form 944 but prefers quarterly Forms 941 must contact the IRS during the first calendar quarter of the tax year to ask for that. Written notice from the IRS to file quarterly Forms 941 in place of Form 944 is required before the employer may file them. The page refers to the Instructions for Form 944 for the methods and deadlines for making a request.

**Correcting Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944 (p. 5).** An error found on a previously filed Form 941 is corrected with Form 941-X, one on Form 943 with Form 943-X, and one on Form 944 with Form 944-X. These X forms are filed separately from the original forms, and employers use them, rather than Form 843, to claim refunds or abatements of employment taxes. The page refers to section 13.

**Zero wage return (p. 6).** Where you have not filed a “final” Form 940 and a “final” Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, or you are not a “seasonal” employer (Form 941 only), you must continue filing Form 940 and Forms 941, Form 943 or Form 944, even for periods in which you paid no wages. The IRS encourages electronic filing of these “zero wage” returns, and the page points to IRS.gov/EmploymentEfile.

**Federal tax deposits must be made by electronic funds transfer (EFT) (p. 6).**
- All federal tax deposits must be made by EFT. An EFT can be made using the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS), IRS Direct Pay or your IRS business tax account.
- If you prefer not to use one of those methods, a tax professional, financial institution, payroll service or other trusted third party can be arranged to make electronic deposits on your behalf. Your financial institution can also be arranged to initiate a same-day wire payment on your behalf.
- EFTPS is a free service of the Department of the Treasury, and payments through IRS Direct Pay or your IRS business tax account are free too. A tax professional, financial institution, payroll service or other third party may charge a fee.
- The page refers to How To Deposit in section 11 for more on making federal tax deposits, to IRS.gov/DirectPay for IRS Direct Pay, and to IRS.gov/BusinessAccount for a payment through your IRS business tax account. For more on EFTPS or to enroll, it gives EFTPS.gov and the numbers 800-555-4477, 800-244-4829 (Spanish) and 303-967-5916 (toll call). A person who is deaf, hard of hearing or has a speech disability can reach EFTPS through Telecommunications Relay Services (TRS) by dialing 711 and then giving the TRS assistant the number 800-555-4477 or 800-733-4829. Pub. 966 has additional information about EFTPS.

**Residents of the Philippines working in the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands (CNMI) (p. 6).** Employers must withhold and pay social security and Medicare taxes on wages and other compensation paid for services as employees in the CNMI to residents of the Philippines who do not hold an H-2 status, unless those workers qualify for exemption from the taxes under an exception listed in section 15. The page refers to Announcement 2012-43, 2012-51 I.R.B. 723.

**Federal employers in the CNMI (p. 6).** In December 2006 the U.S. Treasury Department and the CNMI Division of Revenue and Taxation made an agreement under 5 U.S.C. section 5517. Under it, all federal employers (the Department of Defense included) must withhold CNMI income taxes instead of federal income taxes, and deposit them with the CNMI Treasury. This applies to employees who are subject to CNMI taxes and have their regular place of federal employment in the CNMI. The page points to IRS.gov/5517Agreements for more, including details on completing Form W-2. Federal employers must also file quarterly and annual reports with the CNMI Division of Revenue and Taxation, and questions go to that division.

**Pub. 5146 explains employment tax examinations and appeal rights (p. 6).** Pub. 5146 tells employers how the IRS picks employment tax returns for examination, what happens in an exam, and what options an employer has in responding to its results, including how to appeal. It also covers worker classification issues and tip exams.

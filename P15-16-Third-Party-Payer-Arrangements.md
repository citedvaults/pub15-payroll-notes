## Note
**Outsourcing payroll (p. 53).** An employer may hand some or all of its federal employment tax withholding, reporting and payment obligations to someone else. An employer that outsources these payroll and related tax duties (withholding, reporting and paying over social security, Medicare, FUTA and income taxes) to a third-party payer generally stays responsible for them, liability for the taxes included. The page points to Certified professional employer organization (CPEO), later in the section, for an exception.

**What an employer that outsources should consider (p. 53).**
- The employer stays responsible for federal tax deposits and other federal tax payments, even if it forwards the tax amounts to the third-party payer to make them. When the third party fails to make them, the IRS may assess penalties and interest on the employer's account, and the employer may be liable for all taxes, penalties and interest due. Personal liability for certain unpaid federal taxes is also possible.
- When there are issues with the employer's account, the IRS sends correspondence to the address of record. The page strongly recommends that the employer keep its own address as the address of record, because correspondence going to the third-party payer's address may significantly limit the employer's ability to learn of tax matters involving its business. Form 8822-B is used to update the business address.
- When a third party enrolls an employer in EFTPS for federal tax deposits, the employer receives an Inquiry PIN. The employer should activate and use that PIN to monitor its account and to check that the third party is making the required deposits.

**Common third-party payers (p. 53).** The page lists five that an employer may contract with for payroll and related tax duties: a payroll service provider (PSP), a reporting agent, an agent with an approved Form 2678, a payer designated under section 3504, and a certified professional employer organization (CPEO).

**Payroll service provider (PSP) (p. 53).** A PSP helps run payroll and payroll-related tax duties for the employer, working under the employer's EIN. It may prepare paychecks, prepare and file employment tax returns, prepare Forms W-2, and make federal tax deposits and other federal tax payments. A PSP is not liable, as an employer or as the employer's agent, for the employer's employment taxes. An employer using a PSP remains liable for its employment tax obligations, including liability for employment taxes. The employer should make sure that the PSP uses EFTPS for the federal tax deposits it makes for the employer, so that the employer can confirm payments are being made on its behalf.

**Reporting agent (p. 53).** A reporting agent is a type of PSP. It helps run payroll and payroll-related tax duties for the employer, including authority to sign and file electronically the forms listed on Form 8655, which an employer uses to authorize a reporting agent to act for it. It works under the employer's EIN. A reporting agent is not liable, as an employer or as the employer's agent, for the employer's employment taxes. An employer using one remains liable for its employment obligations, including liability for employment taxes. A reporting agent must use EFTPS for the federal tax deposits it makes for an employer, and the employer has access to EFTPS to confirm those deposits. The page refers to Revenue Procedure 2012-32 and to Pub. 1474 for more on reporting agents.

**Agent with an approved Form 2678 (p. 53).** Such an agent helps run payroll and related tax duties for the employer. An agent authorized under section 3504 may pay wages or compensation to some or all of an employer's employees, prepare and file employment tax returns as Form 2678 sets out, prepare Forms W-2, and make federal tax deposits and other federal tax payments. The employer asks for authority to appoint the agent by using Form 2678. The agent acts under its own EIN. It files Schedule R (Form 941) or, if applicable, Schedule R (Form 943), which spreads wages, taxes and credits claimed among the employers it represents. The agent and an employer that uses it are jointly liable for the employment taxes and related tax duties the agent is authorized to perform. Form 2678 does not reach FUTA taxes reportable on Form 940, except where the employer is a home care service recipient who receives home care services through a program run by a federal, state or local government agency. The page refers to Revenue Procedure 2013-39 for more on such an agent.

**Payer designated under section 3504 (p. 54).** In certain circumstances the IRS may designate a third-party payer to perform the acts of an employer, and it will do so for an employer if the third party has a service agreement with that employer. In a service agreement the third-party payer (1) asserts that it is the employer of the individuals performing services for the employer, (2) pays wages to those individuals, and (3) takes on responsibility for withholding, reporting and paying federal employment taxes on the wages it pays them. The designated payer performs its tax duties under the service agreement using its own EIN. Where the IRS designates a payer under section 3504, the payer and the employer are jointly liable for the employment taxes and related tax duties for which the payer is designated. The page refers to Regulations section 31.3504-2.

**Certified professional employer organization (CPEO) (p. 54).** The Stephen Beck, Jr., ABLE Act of 2014 required the IRS to set up a voluntary certification program for professional employer organizations (PEOs). PEOs take on various payroll administration and tax reporting duties for business clients and are typically paid a fee based on payroll costs. A CPEO must meet various requirements, set out in sections 3511 and 7705 and related published guidance, to become and stay certified. Certification may affect the employment tax liabilities of the CPEO and of its customers. For employment tax purposes a CPEO is generally treated as the employer of an individual who does work for one of its customers under a contract between the CPEO and that customer that section 7705(e)(2) describes (a CPEO contract). This treatment covers only wages and other compensation the CPEO pays that individual. For certain employees covered by a CPEO contract, the page says the reader ("you") may also be treated as an employer of those employees and may be liable for federal employment taxes on wages and other compensation the CPEO pays to them. When filing an aggregate Form 940, Form 941 or Form 943, a CPEO completes Schedule R (Form 940), Schedule R (Form 941) or Schedule R (Form 943), respectively. A CPEO files Form 8973 to notify the IRS when it starts or ends a service contract with a customer. An organization becomes a CPEO by applying through the IRS Online Registration System. The page refers to IRS.gov/CPEO and to Revenue Procedure 2023-18.

**Tip (p. 54).** When an employer and a section 3504 authorized agent (or CPEO or other third-party payer) each paid wages to an employee during a quarter, each should file Form 941 for the wages it paid to the employee in that quarter and should issue Forms W-2 for the wages it paid to the employee during the year.

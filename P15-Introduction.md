## Note
**What the publication covers (p. 12).** The publication sets out an employer’s tax responsibilities. It speaks to agricultural employers, and to employers whose principal place of business is in American Samoa, Guam, the CNMI, the USVI or Puerto Rico. It covers the requirements for withholding, depositing, reporting, paying and correcting employment taxes, and the forms that employers give to employees, that employees give to employers, and that employers send to the IRS and the SSA. “Income tax,” as used in the guide, means federal income tax only, and state or local rules must be checked with your state or local tax department. The term “United States” in the publication includes American Samoa, Guam, the CNMI, the USVI and Puerto Rico, unless otherwise noted.

**Trust fund taxes (p. 12).** Employees are not paid everything they earned, because the employer withholds taxes from their paychecks. The federal income tax and the employees’ share of social security and Medicare taxes withheld form part of the employees’ wages, and they go to the U.S. Treasury rather than to the employees. Employees trust the employer to get the withheld taxes to the U.S. Treasury through federal tax deposits, and that is why the withheld taxes are called trust fund taxes. The trust fund recovery penalty may apply if federal income, social security or Medicare tax that must be withheld is not withheld, or is not deposited or paid to the U.S. Treasury. The page refers to section 11 for more information.

**FUTA tax (p. 12).** The publication also gives employers (employers in the USVI and Puerto Rico included) a summary of their responsibilities under the Federal Unemployment Tax Act, known as FUTA tax. The page refers to section 14.

**Other publications on employment tax (p. 12).** More employment tax information is in Pubs. 15-A, 15-B and 15-T. Pub. 15-A supplements the basic employment tax information of this publication with specialized information. Pub. 15-B, Employer’s Tax Guide to Fringe Benefits, covers the employment tax treatment and valuation of various types of noncash compensation. Pub. 15-T has the federal income tax withholding tables and the instructions for using them.

**The employment taxes (p. 12).** Most employers must withhold (except FUTA), deposit, report and pay four employment taxes: income tax, social security tax, Medicare tax and FUTA tax. The requirements have exceptions, and the page refers to section 15 for guidance. The Instructions for Form CT-1 explain railroad retirement taxes.

**Comments and suggestions (pp. 12–13).** The IRS welcomes comments on the publication and suggestions for future editions. They can be sent through IRS.gov/FormComments, or by mail to Internal Revenue Service, Tax Forms and Publications, 1111 Constitution Ave. NW, IR-6526, Washington, DC 20224. The IRS can’t respond individually to each comment received, but it appreciates the feedback and will weigh the comments and suggestions in revising its tax forms, instructions and publications. Tax questions, tax returns and payments must not be sent to that address.

**Getting answers to your tax questions (p. 13).** For a tax question the publication doesn’t answer, check IRS.gov and How To Get Tax Help at the end of the publication.

**Getting and ordering tax forms, instructions and publications (p. 13).** IRS.gov/Forms has current and prior-year forms, instructions and publications to download. To order current ones, go to IRS.gov/OrderForms; for prior-year forms and instructions, call 800-829-3676. The IRS will process an order as soon as possible; requests already sent should not be resubmitted. Forms and publications can be had faster online.

**Federal government employers (p. 13).** The publication’s information, including the rules for making federal tax deposits, applies to federal agencies.

**State and local government employers (p. 13).** Payments for services in the employ of state and local governments are generally subject to federal income tax withholding but not FUTA tax. Under common-law rules, most elected and appointed public officials of a state or local government are employees; the page refers to chapter 3 of Pub. 963, Federal-State Reference Guide. With certain exceptions, wages are subject to social security and Medicare taxes as well, and the page refers to section 15 for the exceptions. For an election worker employed in another capacity with the same government entity, it refers to Revenue Ruling 2000-6 on page 512 of Internal Revenue Bulletin 2000-6. Information on reporting and social security coverage is available from your local IRS office. A question about coverage under a section 218 (Social Security Act) agreement goes to the appropriate state official; the National Conference of State Social Security Administrators website, NCSSSA.org, is where to find your State Social Security Administrator.

**Indian tribal governments (p. 13).** The page refers to Pub. 4268 for employment tax information for Indian tribal governments.

**Disregarded entities and qualified subchapter S subsidiaries (QSubs) (p. 13).** For employment tax purposes, eligible single-owner disregarded entities and QSubs count as separate entities. An eligible single-member entity reports and pays employment taxes on wages paid to its employees under the entity’s own name and EIN. The page refers to Regulations sections 1.1361-4(a)(7) and 301.7701-2(c)(2)(iv).

**Useful Items (p. 13).** Under this printed heading, “You may want to see,” the page lists these publications: 15-A, Employer’s Supplemental Tax Guide; 15-B, Employer’s Tax Guide to Fringe Benefits; 15-T, Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods; 225, Farmer’s Tax Guide; 583, Starting a Business and Keeping Records; and 1635, Employer Identification Number: Understanding Your EIN.

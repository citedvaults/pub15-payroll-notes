## Note
**What it is and where it is reported (p. 8).** Nonpayroll federal income tax withholding (reported on Forms 1099 and Form W-2G, Certain Gambling Winnings) has to be reported on Form 945 (Annual Return of Withheld Federal Income Tax). Payroll withholding (Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944) and nonpayroll withholding (Form 945) require separate deposits.

**Nonpayroll items (p. 8).** The items include:
- military retirement;
- gambling winnings;
- Indian gaming profits;
- pensions, annuities and IRA distributions, the pensions including distributions from tax-favored retirement plans such as section 401(k), section 403(b) and governmental section 457(b) plans;
- certain government payments for which the recipient elected voluntary federal income tax withholding;
- dividends and other distributions by an ANC for which the recipient elected voluntary federal income tax withholding;
- payments subject to backup withholding.

On depositing and reporting nonpayroll federal income tax withholding, the page refers to the Instructions for Form 945.

**Distributions from nonqualified pension plans and deferred compensation plans (pp. 8–9).** Some nonqualified pension plans and deferred compensation plans (section 457(b) plans of tax-exempt organizations included) pay distributions to participants that are treated as wages and reported on Form W-2. The federal income tax withheld from them is reported on Form 941, Form 943 or Form 944, not on Form 945. A distribution from such a plan to a beneficiary or to the estate of a deceased employee is not wages and is reported on Forms 1099-R, and the federal income tax withheld from it is reported on Form 945.

**Backup withholding (p. 9).** Generally, 24% must be withheld from certain taxable payments when the payee does not give you their correct TIN; this is called “backup withholding.” It applies to interest, dividends, patronage dividends, rents, royalties, commissions, nonemployee compensation, payments in settlement of payment card or third-party network transactions, and certain other payments made in your trade or business. Transactions by brokers and barter exchanges, and certain payments by fishing boat operators, are subject to it too.

**Form W-9 (p. 9).** Form W-9 can be used to ask payees for their TINs. It must be used when payees need to certify that the number given is correct, or need to certify that they are not subject to backup withholding or are exempt from it. The Instructions for the Requester of Form W-9 list the types of payees exempt from backup withholding, and the page refers to Pub. 1281, Backup Withholding for Missing and Incorrect Name/TIN(s), for more.

**Caution: where backup withholding doesn’t apply (p. 9).** The following are outside backup withholding: real estate transactions, long-term-care benefits, health savings accounts (HSAs), medical savings accounts (MSAs), section 404(k) distributions from an employee stock ownership plan (ESOP), IRAs (simplified employee pension (SEP) and SIMPLE retirement plans included), annuities, pensions and wages.

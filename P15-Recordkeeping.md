## Note
**How long to keep records (p. 9).** Records of employment taxes are to be kept for at least 4 years and be available for IRS review.

**What the records should include (p. 9).** The page lists these items:
- your EIN;
- the amounts and dates of all wage, annuity and pension payments;
- the amounts of tips your employees reported to you, and records of allocated tips;
- the fair market value (FMV) of in-kind wages paid;
- the names, addresses, SSNs and occupations of employees and recipients;
- any employee copies of Forms W-2 and W-2c that came back to you as undeliverable;
- the dates of employment for each employee;
- for periods when employees and recipients were paid while absent due to sickness or injury, those periods, and the amount and weekly rate of the payments made to them by you or third-party payers;
- copies of employees’ and recipients’ federal income tax withholding certificates (Forms W-4, W-4P, W-4R, W-4S and W-4V);
- the dates and amounts of your tax deposits, with acknowledgment numbers for deposits made by EFTPS;
- copies of returns filed, with confirmation numbers;
- records of fringe benefits and expense reimbursements provided to employees, including substantiation;
- documentation substantiating any credits claimed.

**Longer periods for certain records (p. 9).**
- Records on qualified sick leave wages and qualified family leave wages (leave taken after March 31, 2021, and before October 1, 2021) should be kept for at least 6 years. The page points to IRS.gov/PLC for the substantiation requirements.
- For qualified wages under the COVID-19 related employee retention credit that were paid after June 30, 2021, the related records should be kept for at least 7 years. The page points to IRS.gov/ERC for the substantiation requirements.

**Crew leaders (p. 9).** If a crew leader furnished you with farmworkers, you must keep a record of the crew leader’s name, permanent mailing address and EIN. If the crew leader has no permanent mailing address, record their present address.

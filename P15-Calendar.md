## Note
**What the list is (pp. 10–11).** The Calendar lists important dates and responsibilities. Its dates are not adjusted for Saturdays, Sundays or legal holidays; Pub. 509, Tax Calendars (for use in 2026), makes that adjustment. For depositing taxes reported on Form 941, Form 943, Form 944 and Form 945, the page refers to section 11, and for depositing FUTA tax it refers to section 14. Due dates for forms required for health coverage reporting are not listed, and the page refers to Pub. 509 for those dates.

**Weekends and holidays (p. 11).** When a date for filing a return, furnishing a form or depositing taxes lands on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the due date becomes the next business day. A “legal holiday” is any legal holiday in the District of Columbia. A statewide legal holiday pushes back a filing due date only when the IRS office where you must file is in that state, but it does not delay the due date of federal tax deposits; the page refers to Deposits Due on Business Days Only in section 11. For any filing due date, the “file” or “furnish” requirement is met if the envelope is properly addressed, carries sufficient postage and is postmarked by the USPS on or before the due date, or if an IRS-designated private delivery service (PDS) sends it on or before that date. The page refers to Private Delivery Services (PDSs) under Reminders, earlier.

**Fiscal-year taxpayers (p. 11).** The due dates listed apply whether you use a calendar year or a fiscal year.

**By January 31 (p. 11)**
- **File Form 941 or Form 944.** Form 941 for the fourth quarter of the previous calendar year is filed, and any undeposited income, social security and Medicare taxes are deposited. Those taxes may instead be paid with Form 941 if total tax liability for the quarter (Form 941, line 12) is under $2,500. Where the IRS has notified you in writing to file Form 944, you file that form for the previous calendar year in place of Form 941 and pay any undeposited taxes with it. Those taxes may be paid with Form 944 if total tax liability for the year (Form 944, line 9) is under $2,500. For further rules on paying with a return, the page refers to Payment with return in section 11. If you timely deposited all taxes when due, you may file by February 10.
- **File Form 943.** This is the agricultural employer’s return for the previous calendar year. Income, social security and Medicare taxes still undeposited are deposited, or may be paid with the form if total tax liability for the year (Form 943, line 13) is under $2,500. If you timely deposited all taxes when due, you may file by February 10.
- **File Form 945.** This form reports any nonpayroll federal income tax withheld. A filer that deposited all taxes when due may file by February 10. The page refers to Nonpayroll Federal Income Tax Withholding under Reminders, earlier.
- **File Form 940.** This form reports any FUTA tax. A filer that deposited all the FUTA tax when due may file by February 10. The page refers to section 14.
- **Furnish Forms 1099 and W-2.** Each employee gets a completed 2025 Form W-2, and payees of nonemployee compensation get a 2025 Form 1099-NEC. Most Forms 1099 must go to payees by January 31, although some may go by February 15, and the page refers to the Guide to Information Returns chart in the General Instructions for Certain Information Returns.
- **File Form W-2.** Copy A of all 2025 paper and electronic Forms W-2 goes to the SSA with Form W-3, Transmittal of Wage and Tax Statements. Forms W-2AS, W-2CM, W-2GU and W-2VI travel with Form W-3SS, and Forms 499R-2/W-2PR with Form W-3 (PR). On reporting Form W-2 information to the SSA electronically, the page points to the SSA’s Employer W-2 Filing Instructions & Information webpage at SSA.gov/employer. Filing through the SSA’s Form W-2 Online service means the SSA generates the Form W-3 data from the Form W-2 submission. Copy 1 of Forms W-2AS, W-2CM, W-2GU and W-2VI, plus Form W-3SS, is sent to your local tax department at the address on Form W-3SS; for more on Copy 1, contact that department. CNMI employers should ask their local tax department how to file Copy 1. For filing Forms 499R-2/W-2PR with the Puerto Rico Department of Treasury, the page gives Hacienda.pr.gov and 787-622-0123.
- **File Form 1099-NEC reporting nonemployee compensation.** Copy A of all 2025 paper and electronic Forms 1099-NEC is filed with the IRS, and paper forms go in with Form 1096, Annual Summary and Transmittal of U.S. Information Returns. For electronic filing of information returns with the IRS, the page refers to Pub. 1220.

**By February 15 (p. 11)**
- **Request a new Form W-4 from exempt employees.** Ask every employee who claimed exemption from federal income tax withholding last year for a new Form W-4.

**On February 16 (p. 11)**
- **Forms W-4 claiming exemption from withholding expire.** A Form W-4 that claimed exemption for the previous year has expired. Withholding must start for an employee who claimed exemption before but has not given you a new Form W-4 for the current year. Without a new Form W-4, withhold as though the employee had checked Single or Married filing separately in Step 1(c) of the 2026 Form W-4 and left Steps 2, 3 and 4 blank; the page refers to section 9. If an employee gives you a new Form W-4 claiming exemption after February 15, you may apply the exemption to future wages, but you do not refund taxes withheld while the exempt status was not in place.

**By February 28 (p. 12)**
- **File paper 2025 Forms 1099 and 1096.** Copy A of all paper 2025 Forms 1099 other than Forms 1099-NEC is filed with the IRS together with Form 1096. For returns filed electronically, the page refers to By March 31, later.
- **File paper Form 8027.** Form 8027, Employer’s Annual Information Return of Tip Income and Allocated Tips, is filed on paper with the IRS. The page refers to section 6, and to By March 31 for electronically filed returns.

**By March 31 (p. 12)**
- **File electronic 2025 Forms 1099 and 8027.** Electronic 2025 Forms 1099 other than Forms 1099-NEC go to the IRS, and so does electronic Form 8027. For filing information returns electronically with the IRS, the page refers to Pub. 1220 and to Pub. 1239, Specifications for Electronic Filing of Form 8027.

**By April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31 (p. 12)**
- **Deposit FUTA tax.** The quarter’s FUTA tax, including any amount carried over from other quarters, is deposited if it is over $500; at $500 or less it is carried over to the next quarter. The page refers to section 14.
- **File Form 941.** Form 941 is filed and any undeposited income, social security and Medicare taxes are deposited. These taxes may be paid with the form if total tax liability for the quarter (Form 941, line 12) is under $2,500. Where all taxes were timely deposited when due, filing may wait until May 10, August 10, November 10 or February 10, respectively. A business that has been notified to file Form 944, and did not ask for and receive written IRS notice to file quarterly Forms 941, does not file Form 941 for these quarters.

**Before December 1 (p. 12)**
- **New Forms W-4.** Employees should be reminded to send in a new Form W-4 when their filing status, other income, deductions or credits have changed, or will change, for the next year. They should also be reminded to do so if they made a mid-year change to their Form W-4 using the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator, available at IRS.gov/W4App. An employee who made a mid-year change may be underwithheld or overwithheld once the Form W-4 applies to the next full calendar year.

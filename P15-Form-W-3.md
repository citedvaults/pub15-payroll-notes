## Note
- Unless otherwise noted, the publication’s references to Form W-3 also cover Form W-3SS and Form W-3 (PR) (F04, p. 3).
- Form W-3 is printed as Transmittal of Wage and Tax Statements; under the Calendar’s By January 31 date, you send the SSA Copy A of every 2025 Form W-2, paper or electronic, together with Form W-3 (F21, p. 11).
- Forms W-2AS, W-2CM, W-2GU and W-2VI go with Form W-3SS, and Forms 499R-2/W-2PR go with Form W-3 (PR) (F21, p. 11).
- When you file through the SSA’s Form W-2 Online service, the SSA produces the Form W-3 data itself from the Form(s) W-2 you submit electronically (F21, p. 11).
- Copy 1 of Forms W-2AS, W-2CM, W-2GU and W-2VI, together with Form W-3SS, goes to your local tax department at the address printed on Form W-3SS (F21, p. 11).
- In place of ordering paper Forms W-2 and W-3, the page suggests considering the SSA’s free e-file service, and says Form W-3 is created for you from your Forms W-2 (F19, p. 10).
- The IRS runs the TSO to answer questions about reporting on Forms W-2, W-3 and 1099 and on other information returns (F08, p. 7).
- The Employer Responsibilities list, under Annually (see Calendar for due dates), includes filing Copy A of Forms W-2 with the transmittal Form W-3 to the SSA, and reconciling Forms 941 (or Form 943 or Form 944) with Forms W-2 and W-3 (F09, p. 8).
- An employer that must file a final return must also file Forms W-2 and W-3 with the SSA, by the final return’s due date (S12, p. 39).
- Discrepancies between Forms 941, 943 or 944 filed with the IRS and Forms W-2 and W-3 filed with the SSA may lead the IRS or the SSA to contact you (S12, p. 39).
- To reduce discrepancies, report social security and Medicare wages and taxes separately, both on Forms W-2 and W-3 and on Forms 941, 943 or 944 (S12, p. 40).
- If an EIN you used on a Form 941, 943 or 944 during the year differs from the EIN reported on Form W-3, put the other EIN in the Form W-3 box “Other EIN used this year” (box h); the matching box on Form W-3 (PR) is box f (S12, p. 40).
- The amounts on Form W-3 must add up to the amounts from your Forms W-2 (S12, p. 40).
- Form W-3 is reconciled with your four quarterly Forms 941, or your annual Form 943 or Form 944, by comparing federal income tax withheld, social security and Medicare wages, and social security and Medicare taxes (S12, p. 40).
- Generally the social security and Medicare taxes on Forms 941, 943 or 944 (current year adjustments included) come to roughly double those on Form W-3, since the returns count the employer share and the employee share while Form W-3 counts only the employee share (S12, p. 40).
- Amounts on Forms W-2, W-3 and Forms 941, 943 or 944 can differ for valid reasons (S12, p. 40).
- Where Additional Medicare Tax was withheld, the Medicare tax on Form 941 (line 5c, column 2), Form 943 (line 5) or Form 944 (line 4c, column 2) will not be double the Medicare tax withheld shown in box 6 of Form W-3 (box 13 of Form W-3 (PR)), because that tax falls on the employee alone (S12, p. 40).
- Under the heading Correcting filed Forms W-2 and W-3, the page says a changed wage total for a previous year, which leads to adjusting wages and social security and Medicare taxes, also means filing Form W-2c and Form W-3c with the SSA (S13.02, p. 43).
